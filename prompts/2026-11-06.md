### NATO opens a counter-drone marketplace as Shield AI agrees to buy Echodyne
NATO's communications agency has launched a marketplace where allied nations can buy from a list of 16 pre-approved counter-drone systems, without having to hold tenders of their own, AeroTime reports. An additional 3.1 million euros has been earmarked for more testing. The French company Alta Ares, which develops interceptors powered by artificial intelligence, says it secured six of the 16 lots. In a separate deal, Shield AI, the company behind the Hivemind AI pilot, has agreed to acquire radar manufacturer Echodyne to bolster its counter-drone offerings. Drones have pushed rapid, AI-assisted air defence up military agendas, and NATO is now buying that capability off a prepared catalogue, much as governments purchase software.

### Russia and US seek to block plan for binding rules on autonomous weapons
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/nov/06/autonomous-weapons-ai-russia-us-binding-rules

A group of countries led by Russia and the United States is working to derail talks on legally binding rules for autonomous weapons, the Guardian reports. ### VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08449

In a paper titled VFlash: Fast, Accurate Visual Planning via Flash Primitive Decoding, the researchers take on a speed problem in the AI that guides robots and self-driving cars. These systems can plan visually as well as in words, marking targets and predicting movement using points, boxes drawn around objects, and paths. Current vision-language models, which pair image understanding with text, produce such plans one coordinate at a time, so more detailed plans take longer. VFlash takes advantage of the predictable shapes of these visual elements to work out all of their coordinates at once, relying on a small assistant model that sketches an entire element in a single step.

### OpenAI launches GPT-6 Cyber and rates it "Critical" for cyber risk
Lead: yes
OpenAI has launched GPT-6 Cyber, a model built to discover and confirm exploitable weaknesses in software while keeping within the boundaries a security researcher defines. Under its own safety framework, the company classifies the model's cyber capability as "Critical". As a result, access is currently limited to chosen customers in its Daybreak Red programme, via the API and Codex, though OpenAI intends to broaden availability over the next few weeks. According to the company, the model uncovered 81 high-severity vulnerabilities in Firefox that had not been known before. It also achieved 48.5 percent on OpenAI's ExploitGym benchmark, compared with 32.1 percent for GPT-5.6 Cyber. Those results come from OpenAI itself. Tools able to spot serious software weaknesses can help defenders but also pose risks if misused, and restricting this one to vetted security teams will test whether limited access can manage that tension.

### METR finds AI is helping Anthropic improve its models about twice as fast
The independent evaluator METR has released an assessment of Anthropic's internal research covering August to October. METR estimates that the capabilities of Anthropic's models are advancing roughly 1.9 times faster than they did between March 2024 and October 2025. It says AI tools that accelerate employees' coding and experimental work likely explain most of that gain. METR said it saw no obvious constraint that would prevent further acceleration within the next year. However, Anthropic blacked out much of the evidence METR regarded as most revealing, pointing to competitive confidentiality, which means the full grounds for the findings cannot be verified publicly. If AI is now hastening the creation of its own successors, progress could speed up sharply, raising the stakes for independent measurement and openness.

# CHINA

### China cracks down on AI fakes that impersonate celebrities
The Cyberspace Administration of China, the country's internet regulator, says it has taken down more than 47,000 posts and punished 3,961 accounts that used artificial intelligence to pose as celebrities, China Daily reports. The crackdown focuses on AI-generated faces and voices used to deceive the public or promote products. With convincing deepfakes now cheap to produce, impersonation of famous people has become a large-scale problem, and regulators are beginning to target the accounts behind it as well as the content itself.

# INDIA

### India's CERT-In asks organisations to audit what their AI agents can access and do
CERT-In, India's national cybersecurity agency, has urged organisations to check whether their AI agents could leak confidential data or take actions they were never permitted to perform, The Times of India reports. The agency's AI security auditing guidelines call for strict controls on permissions, a log of every step an agent takes, an emergency mechanism to halt an agent, and sign-off from a human before any significant action. The guidance also addresses prompt injection, in which harmful instructions concealed in documents, emails or web pages attempt to hijack an agent. Indian companies are beginning to let AI agents reach email, files, internal systems and financial data, and the guidelines treat agent permissions and audit records as a core security safeguard from the outset.


Source: The Times of India https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/business/india-business/cert-in-seeks-checks-on-access-of-ai-agents/articleshow/135589474.cms
