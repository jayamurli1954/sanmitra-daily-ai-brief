### From Pixels to Actions - Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06918

Researchers are rethinking how robots turn sight into movement in a paper titled From Pixels to Actions, Towards Native Vision-Language-Action Policies. These models combine camera images, written instructions and physical actions. Usually a separate visual encoder first condenses an image into a summary, and only then does the system reason about what to do. The researchers argue this can discard the details that handling objects depends on, such as edges, depth, shape and small changes. Their system, NEO-VLA, drops the separate encoder. Light processing layers break images into pieces that are handled together with language, letting the action component draw on visual information at several stages.

### Moonshot AI releases Kimi K3.1 Pro model weights
Source: TechNode
https://technode.com/2026/11/05/moonshot-ai-releases-kimi-k3-1-pro-model-weights/

Moonshot AI has published the weights of Kimi K3.1 Pro on Hugging Face, TechNode reports, so organisations can now run the company's flagship multimodal model on their own. The model uses a mixture-of-experts design, with 2.8 trillion parameters in total and roughly 104 billion active for each inference. It can handle a context window of about one million tokens. Compared with Kimi K3, the new version adds more reinforcement learning aimed at coding, 3D creation, financial research and lengthy agent tasks. The model was previously offered only through Kimi's apps and API. Huawei said it will support inference through vLLM Ascend and SGLang on its Atlas A3 and Ascend 950 systems.

### Nothing plans December launch for first AI-native device
Source: TechNode
https://technode.com/2026/11/05/nothing-plans-december-launch-for-first-ai-native-device/

Nothing will release its first AI-native hardware product in December, founder and chief executive Carl Pei said on November 4, according to TechNode. Pei called the device a screenless productivity tool and said an app on its own could not have provided what it does. In an essay called Toolmakers, he argued that AI tools miss much of the context around people's work, such as conversations and ideas that never get typed into a chatbot. He suggested ordinary hardware could help fill that gap. The company has not revealed the device's exact form, specifications or price.
