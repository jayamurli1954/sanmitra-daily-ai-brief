https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/nov/05/albanese-government-warned-its-sleepwalking-into-another-robodebt-scenario-over-use-of-algorithms-and-ai

An independent think tank has warned that Australia's federal government risks a repeat of the robodebt scandal, the Guardian reports. Dr Catherine Williams, chief executive of the Centre for Public Integrity, said the government was sleepwalking toward that outcome because there is little transparency and no legislation covering how algorithms and artificial intelligence shape official decisions. On Thursday she released the centre's report, titled Computer Says No. It reviews the government's reliance on automated decision-making tools and urges urgent reform. The centre fears Australians may not know when such systems have played a part in decisions about them. The report makes eight recommendations. They include a compulsory audit and a public register covering every use of automated decision-making, along with an independent watchdog with powers to monitor, audit and investigate the government. Williams said the work grew out of the centre's mounting concern over an algorithm in the integrated assessment tool, which determines how much home support older Australians can receive.

Some artificial intelligence data centers are generating their own electricity with converted jet engines while they wait years for grid connections and new turbines, TechCrunch reports, citing the New York Times. The engines are taken from retired aircraft and rebuilt to run on natural gas. Nobody sees them as a long-term power source, but they show how far electricity supply has lagged behind the expansion of AI. For companies caught in those queues, the key appeal is how quickly the engines can be put to work. ### Anthropic is working on a system to prove AI models’ identity
Source: TechCrunch
https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/03/anthropic-is-working-on-a-system-to-prove-ai-models-identity/

Anthropic said in a blog post on Tuesday that it is developing an open source system to confirm which AI model generated a particular output. The system is named FlashProve. It aims to deliver what researchers describe as provable inference, meaning a cryptographic proof that acts as a mathematical guarantee that a response was produced by a specific model. The goal is to let users check that an answer genuinely came from the model they believe they are using. Cryptographic email signatures let recipients verify who really sent a message, and FlashProve works in a similar way. It is designed to stop anyone passing one model off as another, even if the deception comes from the lab that built and operates the model. The researchers say it could prevent an AI company from quietly substituting a cheaper model to save money, or a pricier one to boost its performance results.

### ‘Discontent and distrust’: annual social cohesion checkup shows exactly what’s making Australia angry
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/australia-news/2026/nov/04/social-cohesion-report-australia-cost-of-living-economy-immigration-multiculturalism-ai-ntwnfb

Most Australians still support immigration and multiculturalism, even as more of them feel financially worse off and grow gloomier and more distrustful of government, the Guardian reports, citing the annual Scanlon report on social cohesion. Respondents are more anxious about artificial intelligence than about climate change or war. Their top worries, though, are a global economic slowdown and the cost of living.
