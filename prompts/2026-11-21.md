# WORLD

### International talks on controlling use of autonomous weapons collapse
Source: The Guardian
https://is.gd/3WO8Ri

An effort to open negotiations on a legally binding international treaty on autonomous weapons has failed, the Guardian reports. The talks took place over a week at the Review Conference on Conventional Weapons, held at the United Nations in Geneva. After negotiating through the night, delegates gave up shortly before 5am on Saturday because they could not overcome objections from Russia, which was accused of filibustering. A large majority of countries had wanted a mandate that would lead to a binding treaty on AI warfare. But decisions at the conference require consensus, so delegates had to settle for a much weaker option.

### ‘Little room for error’: what a debt-laden cloud computing company says about the AI boom
Source: The Guardian
https://is.gd/A8qYcK

CoreWeave, a cloud computing company carrying heavy debt, has been called the poster child of an AI bubble. ### In a year of AI solving maths problems, an Anthropic worker says he may have cracked the biggest
Source: BBC
https://is.gd/h46Iyo
Also: The Economic Times https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow/136045658.cms

A mathematician who works at Anthropic says he used the company's AI chatbot to prove the Riemann hypothesis, the BBC reports. Levent Alpöge made the claim on Friday, one day after Anthropic's shares began trading on the US stock market. He said he spent about six weeks working with the Claude chatbot to build a detailed proof of the famous problem. If the proof holds up, it would rank among the most important in the history of mathematics. Alpöge called it "one dawn across science of something far greater." The announcement comes after months in which AI companies, OpenAI among them, have announced dozens of solutions to difficult problems in advanced mathematics that scholars had failed to solve for decades. The proof has not yet been confirmed as correct.

### ‘I’m opening the cage of a monster’: Taxi Driver scriptwriter Paul Schrader on unleashing AI at 80
Source: The Guardian
https://is.gd/QjxyuE

Paul Schrader, the 80-year-old filmmaker behind Taxi Driver, Raging Bull and American Gigolo, has started using artificial intelligence in his work, the Guardian reports. Speaking over white wine at a restaurant in Greenwich Village, he said that using the technology felt like opening the cage of a monster, one he has no leash to control. Over a long career, Schrader has worked with Martin Scorsese, Robert De Niro, Sigourney Weaver and Richard Gere. He has written and directed dozens of internationally praised films across half a century. Hollywood's guilds have generally opposed using AI to create synthetic actors, special effects or screenplays, but Schrader is using it for all three. He believes AI will inevitably take over, both in cinema, the art form he has spent his life on, and across humanity more broadly.

### Oracle says OpenAI infrastructure deal is fully funded after final capital raise
Source: The Economic Times
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow/136044027.cms

Oracle said on Friday that its infrastructure agreement with OpenAI is now fully financed, after a final round of fundraising earlier this month. ### Telecoms ramp up AI investment amid questions over returns
Source: The Korea Times
https://is.gd/EQmb7q

South Korea's three main telecom operators are spending more as they move into AI data centers, though it is unclear whether the investment will pay off, The Korea Times reports. SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus spent a combined 3.68 trillion won on capital projects in the first nine months of this year, up 13.7 percent on a year earlier. LG Uplus spent 569.8 billion won on a standalone basis in the third quarter, an increase of 30.1 percent. It is building a 200-megawatt, four-building complex in Paju. The first 50-megawatt building is expected to be finished by next June, and all four are due to be running by 2028. KT's spending, including major affiliates, rose 20.3 percent to 1.599 trillion won. SK Telecom's spending, including SK Hyper, rose 3 percent to 1.234 trillion won, and the company is looking for outside investors to share development costs. Returns depend on customer demand and how fully the centers are used, while building, power, maintenance and equipment are all expensive. The report also notes that US hyperscalers usually write off GPUs over five years, even though new AI chips come out almost every year.

### Machine Gods: Anthropic's early days
Source: NPR
https://is.gd/l8RhBG

After Anthropic completed what NPR describes as the biggest initial public offering ever, the broadcaster's new podcast Machine Gods is looking back at the company's early years. The show, which covers the AI revolution, is hosted by Kevin Roose and Casey Newton. They describe a time just three years ago when Anthropic had fewer than 200 employees working in a crowded office, with no certain future. In those days, the staff were intensely focused on AI safety and treated making money as a secondary concern. The hosts look at why almost nobody expected the company to succeed back then. That start contrasts sharply with this week's record stock market debut. The episode opens with a disclosure from Newton before the hosts look back at the company's origins.

### Daniel Ek backs AI researcher’s fusion power startup. Here’s what they’re building.
Source: TechCrunch
https://is.gd/Oz7SUW

Spotify founder Daniel Ek has invested in Europa Fusion, a low-profile UK startup that is trying a different way to develop a fusion power plant, TechCrunch reports. Bloomberg was the first to report Ek's involvement. The company was founded in February by AI researcher Nantas Nardelli. Sources told TechCrunch that he had been working on the idea for some time before that, speaking anonymously because the details have not been made public. Nardelli has a long background in artificial intelligence, having worked at Google DeepMind, Facebook AI Research and Microsoft Research Cambridge. Most recently, he led machine learning at Pacific Fusion, a fusion startup based in Fremont, California, which has just started building a demonstration facility in New Mexico. Like most fusion companies of its size, Pacific Fusion depends heavily on physical machines to test its ideas and refine its computer simulations.

### Nvidia's accounting is a focus of US SEC investigation, sources say
Source: The Economic Times
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow/136043101.cms

The US Securities and Exchange Commission is examining Nvidia's accounting, according to two people briefed on the matter, adding to the legal problems facing the chipmaker. The Economic Times carried the report, which comes from Reuters. Earlier, on Tuesday, the Wall Street Journal reported that the regulator was investigating Nvidia but said it could not find out what the inquiry was about. The two sources spoke on condition of anonymity because the matter is confidential. They said SEC enforcement staff are looking into possible accounting problems at the company. A spokesperson for the SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia did not immediately reply to requests for comment. Nvidia's shares fell further after the details came out, and were down about 13% on Friday, according to LSEG data. Before the report was published, the stock had been down more than 6%.
