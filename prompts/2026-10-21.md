# WORLD

### One hacker's AI agents broke into more than 100 company websites
Lead: yes
Security researcher Snorre Fagerland of NorForge watched a Chinese-speaking hacker hand almost an entire hacking campaign to AI agents, Reuters reported. Starting 25 September, the agents tried more than 1,200 e-commerce sites in the US, Canada, Australia and Japan from instructions of fewer than 100 Chinese characters, and reached sites belonging to more than 100 companies; Reuters named Deere, SharkNinja and Cummins among them. A successful break-in took about 23 hours, and Fagerland estimates about $5,000 of tokens in total, roughly $50 per hack, apparently using a mix of DeepSeek models and Claude Code. The campaign ran on exposed copies of Cairn, an open-source agent platform. How much data was taken is not known; Deere said it saw no sign sensitive data was accessed and SharkNinja said a standalone support site was hit and contained. The report was published Monday and has not been independently audited. (Themes: technology trends; AI policy and regulation)

# USA

### Washington formally orders DeepSeek out of all civilian federal agencies
The Department of Homeland Security published a notice in the Federal Register on 20 October of an order that bars federal civilian agencies from buying DeepSeek products and makes them remove DeepSeek software, affiliate products and third-party products built on DeepSeek from their IT systems. It was issued under the 2018 Federal Acquisition Supply Chain Security Act after a Federal Acquisition Security Council review. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin's letter cites data stored on servers in China and the risk that prompts could pass sensitive data to the Chinese government. Agencies can seek exceptions with 60 days' notice and a "compelling justification". The order was signed months ago and is only now public, and it does not cover private or personal use. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)

### Meta opens up Prometheus, its gigawatt-scale training cluster
In an engineering post, Meta said the first phase of Prometheus in New Albany, Ohio is complete and trained its Muse models, including Muse Wonder, and that it is scaling the site toward about 1 million GPUs. It reports more than 85% "goodput" (useful training time) on pre-training runs and more than 90% on reinforcement-learning runs, with a mean time between interruptions of about 47,000 GPU hours, since raised above 54,000 after firmware and fleet fixes. These are Meta's own figures. (Theme: technology trends)

# CHINA

### DeepSeek open-sources DeepInfer, the engine behind its own API
DeepSeek said on the afternoon of 20 October that it is fully open-sourcing DeepInfer, the internal framework that schedules its compute clusters to serve users. It says DeepInfer already runs high-throughput, low-latency serving of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and that inference code for future models will be published there, with the aim of cutting compute cost and power use for anyone self-hosting. The release lands the same day as the US federal ban above. Performance claims are DeepSeek's own. (Theme: new AI models and tools)

Source: East Money / Securities Times https://finance.eastmoney.com/news/1345,202610203901464872.html

### Ant Group open-sources five Ming-3.0 speech models
Ant's Bailing team released the Ming-3.0 speech family on 20 October: text-to-speech models at 0.5B and tiny sizes, two audio-understanding models that transcribe, summarise and analyse long recordings, and a tiny speech-recognition model that takes hot-word lists without retraining. Weights are on Hugging Face and ModelScope. Ant cites Artificial Analysis results that put its TTS model joint-first on pronunciation robustness; benchmark figures are company-reported. (Theme: new AI models and tools)

Source: AIbase https://news.aibase.cn/news/31738

### Beijing drafts new policy to fuse AI with manufacturing
At a State Council Information Office briefing on 20 October, MIIT chief engineer Wang Weiming said the ministry is drafting measures for next-generation smart manufacturing, backing industrial datasets, vertical and industry-specific models, an industrial AI operating system and pilot "industrial agents" for scheduling, defect detection and maintenance. No text or timeline has been published yet. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)

# ASIA

### South Korea pitches an AI hub with six development banks
Speaking at the Global AI and Digital Summit in Seoul on 20 October, Finance Minister Lee Hyoung-il said Korea is working with six multilateral development banks, including the World Bank, to set up an AI hub bringing their AI cooperation centres together in Korea, aimed at a "global AI basic society" where no country is left out. He also discussed with World Bank President Ajay Banga sharing Korean nuclear-power know-how to give developing countries stable electricity for AI. The finance ministry put attendance at about 900 people from some 160 countries. It is a stated plan, not a signed agreement. (Themes: AI policy and regulation; technology trends)

Source: The Korea Times (Yonhap) https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/economy/20261020/finance-minister-pledges-support-for-global-ai-basic-society

### SK chairman calls for shared Korea-Japan AI infrastructure
SK Group chairman Chey Tae-won, who also heads the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry, proposed a joint Korea-Japan government task force to build shared AI factories and infrastructure, arguing the power demands of AI make going it alone too costly and that the two economies together form a roughly $6 trillion bloc. He also said reports that SK Hynix would build a joint fab with Kioxia were a misunderstanding. It is a proposal in an interview, not a government plan. (Theme: AI industry and startup news)

# INDIA

### Government maps a sovereign, air-gapped AI compute tier
The government is drawing up a sovereign AI map that sorts sectors and data by sensitivity, The Economic Times reported, citing people familiar with the matter. For critical sectors such as defence, the infrastructure including GPUs would be "on-premises, entirely owned by the government and air-gapped"; for less sensitive but important categories the state may own the hardware while private firms run it. This is reported, not yet an official policy. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)
