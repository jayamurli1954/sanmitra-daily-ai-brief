### cmHC: Balancing Stream Mixing in Hyper-Connections via Geometric Contraction
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27193

The researchers behind a paper titled cmHC: Balancing Stream Mixing in Hyper-Connections via Geometric Contraction look at how information should be blended inside AI models. It builds on an approach called manifold-constrained hyper-connections, which splits a model's shortcut pathways into several parallel streams. To keep training stable, the rules for mixing those streams are restricted to a particular mathematical set of matrices. The researchers found a trade-off. If the streams are mixed too little, they end up acting almost independently. If they are mixed too much, they become hard to tell apart.

### Why data centers are at the center of the Big Tech backlash
Source: TechCrunch
https://is.gd/zJYVfJ

Journalist Paris Marx examines data centers and generative AI in a new book called Hyperscale. In it, he takes readers inside the facilities and traces how computing infrastructure that has existed for decades has recently become politically explosive. The book arrives as local opposition to new data centers grows, according to TechCrunch. Speaking to TechCrunch, Marx called the expansion of data centers "fundamentally unsustainable" and said it was incompatible with a planet people can live on. He also argued that the sites are an easy target for wider frustration with the technology industry, because when one goes up in a community it is a physical, tangible presence. He said this holds true whatever a person's political views.

### How Nvidia's billion-dollar turnaround in China is threatened by Huang's exit
Source: The Economic Times
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow/136101891.cms

After US export bans cost it billions of dollars in sales, Nvidia rebuilt its business in China's fast-growing AI sector by selling less powerful chips and software. That recovery is now at risk from the scandal around former chief executive Jensen Huang, The Economic Times reports. Universities, companies and research institutes in China have been buying more Nvidia products to run AI models for everything from business software to humanoid robots. # INDIA

### L&T Technology Services Launches AI Toolchain for Software-Defined Vehicles
Source: Analytics India Magazine
https://is.gd/bZeo2M

L&T Technology Services, known as LTTS, has introduced an AI-driven engineering toolchain to help companies develop and validate software-defined vehicles, Analytics India Magazine reports. The platform, SDVault.ai, uses more than 250 AI agents across 12 engineering areas. These include requirements, system architecture, functional safety, cybersecurity, software development and testing. It belongs to the company's EI for Engineering suite and runs on its EIOne Engineering Intelligence platform. LTTS said safety-critical work is handled by a mix of AI agents and rule-based automation, and that engineers keep the final say on design decisions, verification and certification. The launch is part of a wider move by services firms to use AI in automotive software. In October, for example, HCLTech launched a validation service for software-defined vehicles powered by NVIDIA, which uses AI agents and virtual simulations to automate parts of the testing process.
