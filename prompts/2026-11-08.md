# WORLD

### ‘AI clog’: UK public bodies battle onslaught of chatbot-generated complaints
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/08/ai-clog-public-bodies-local-authorities-onslaught-chatbot-generated-correspondence

The Guardian reports that public bodies in the UK are struggling with a growing flood of complaints and letters written by AI chatbots. Hollie Wickens is a senior caseworker for a Labour MP. She says these messages began arriving slowly in 2024 and have since become overwhelming. By her estimate, about half of the emails her office gets now involve AI in some way. She has ten years in the job, but she says the trend has made requests and complaints harder and slower to read and handle. "It is definitely more taxing," she said. Earlier this decade, applying for a job, making a complaint or objecting to a local planning proposal took real effort. People often had to do research, fill in forms, write formally and draw on specialist knowledge. That changed as generative AI tools such as ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot and Gemini spread quickly over the past couple of years. ChatGPT turns four later this month.

### Advances in Agentic Computer Graphics
Desk: Research of the Week
Source: arXiv
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.01860

This week's research highlight is a survey titled Advances in Agentic Computer Graphics. It looks at how AI systems built on large language models, the technology behind today's chatbots, are starting to take on work in computer graphics. In graphics, software code sets out the steps for creating and changing images. These language models have become good at writing code, reasoning and using software tools, so they can now work as what are called agents. An agent writes graphics code, runs it in a graphics program, checks the result and uses that feedback to improve its next attempt. The researchers call these agentic graphics systems. Such systems have already done well at building and repeatedly editing 3D objects, animating objects with moving joints, and putting together moving scenes that simulate solid bodies and fluids. For example, a user no longer has to hand-tune the shader programs that control how a surface looks. Instead, they can guide an agent's changes with a short written instruction and, if they like, a reference picture. The researchers say the field is growing so fast that reviewing the research by hand cannot keep up. Different research areas also often use different words for the same ideas. To fix this, they propose one shared system for classifying agentic tools across graphics tasks. They also built their own agent-based tool to help human researchers analyse large numbers of older and newly released papers. With these, they map new trends and point to open problems. These include coordinating several agents and tools, designing ways for people and agents to work together, keeping up long-term planning and refinement, and finding dependable ways to evaluate interactive graphics systems.

# USA

### Megaspeed exec was recruited to spy on Nvidia CEO, according to US intelligence memo
Source: TechCrunch
https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/07/chinese-exec-was-recruited-to-spy-on-nvidia-ceo-according-to-us-intelligence-memo/

A memo that circulated earlier this year claimed that China's Ministry of State Security recruited technology executive Alice Huang to spy on Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang, TechCrunch reports, citing several media outlets. The New York Times first revealed the memo, which was reportedly based on U.S. intelligence. The paper had already reported that Nvidia's CEO was a target of Chinese intelligence. The Financial Times later reported that the memo also alleged a romantic relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a company based in Singapore. An Nvidia spokesperson told the Times that Jensen Huang's dealings with Alice Huang were "not unusual." The spokesperson added that Nvidia does not pass classified information to its customers. In a separate report, the Times examined New Matrix, a Chinese company.

### Amazon's Astro home robot has fans but looks destined for the 'trash heap.' Why it's trying again
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/08/amazon-home-robot-fauna-sprout.html

Amazon is trying again with its Astro home robot, even though there are signs the original may be headed for the scrap pile, CNBC reports. Bezos, the company's founder and then chief executive, is a fan of science fiction and robotics. He believed homes would soon have robots of their own. He told Amazon's senior device executives to build one, and within a few years the team came up with Astro, priced at $1,450. The robot is essentially a tablet on wheels running Alexa. It can move around a home by itself, recognize faces and act as a mobile security monitor. It was first shown to the public on stage at Amazon's fall 2021 event. But Astro stayed invitation-only and reached just a few thousand customers, some of whom now expect it won't last much longer. Amazon also launched a version for businesses in 2023, then shut it down within a year.
