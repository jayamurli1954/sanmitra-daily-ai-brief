### Confucius4-R2T2: A Stable-Prefix Streaming ASR Model for Real Real-Time Transcription
Desk: Research of the Week
Source: arXiv
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06043

Researchers have introduced a speech recognition system built so it never goes back and changes words it has already produced. Their paper is titled Confucius4-R2T2: A Stable-Prefix Streaming ASR Model for Real Real-Time Transcription. ASR stands for automatic speech recognition, and streaming means the transcript is generated while someone is still talking. Live captions, translation software and voice assistants increasingly act on each word the moment it appears. When a recognizer later revises an early word, a problem sometimes called flicker, any work built on it is wasted, and an AI agent might even take the wrong action. The researchers argue that this kind of stability matters just as much as accuracy and speed. To tackle it, they took an existing model, Qwen3-ASR-1.7B, and gave it further training on 1,500 hours of Chinese and English audio, leaving its core audio-processing components frozen. The method rests on an idea they call the Longest Stable Prefix, essentially the opening stretch of a transcript that can safely be committed without later correction. They provide mathematical proof that this measure behaves consistently and matches what the model would output after hearing the complete recording. Training on these labels turns a model designed for finished recordings into a stable live transcriber, with no change to its underlying design. In testing, the system came close to the original model's quality on full recordings while producing steady text with little delay. The improvements also carried over to languages beyond the two used in training.

### South Korea's top scorer in tax accountancy exam admits cheating
Lead: yes
Source: BBC
https://www.bbc.co.uk/news/articles/ckvg2ezvpe08o?at_medium=RSS&at_campaign=rss
Also: The Guardian https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/15/ai-cheating-south-korea-college-entrance-exam

The person who came top in South Korea's certified tax accountant exam has confessed to cheating with a tiny camera, a mobile phone and AI tools, the BBC reports. Han Won-seok, who is 34, made the admission in a video posted on Sunday to the YouTube channel of his lawyer, Lee Don-Ho. He said he had also confessed to the Seoul Metropolitan Police Agency and told officers how he did it. In the first round, Han scored 397.5 out of 400, getting only one of 160 questions wrong. That result raised suspicions and set off speculation online. "I cheated in both the first and second rounds," Han said, adding that he had relied on electronic gadgets and generative AI. In South Korea, national standardised exams have a big influence on people's careers, so their results, and any attempt to boost them unfairly, are taken very seriously.
