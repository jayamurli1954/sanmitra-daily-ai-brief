### 42AI emerges with a model designed to predict human behavior
Lead: yes
An Israeli-American startup called 42AI has made its public debut, TechCrunch reported on 2 November. The company says its system, which it calls a behavioral world model, can estimate how people will react when they face situations they have not met before. Financial brokerages are its first market. The startup has disclosed $62.5 million in seed funding. That money came from its founders and Infinity Group over three years, not in one new round. Its predictive performance is so far only the company's own claim. The widely quoted figure of 98% accuracy measures whether trading flows land within a range. It does not mean every trade is predicted correctly. Profiling individuals this way also raises questions about privacy and conflicts of interest.

### Parents and teachers decry Google pledge to yank Search if schools block AI: ‘This threat is nothing but extortion’
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/nov/09/parents-teachers-google-search-schools-ai

Google is facing criticism from parents and teachers in the United States over a new condition attached to its classroom technology, the Guardian reports. Last week the company released a group of artificial intelligence tools for schools, along with settings that let teachers and administrators limit how those tools are used. Google says each school can decide for itself whether students get access to AI. But there is a condition. Any school that switches off the AI tools will also lose access to Google Search. Chris Phillips, Google's vice-president for education, defended the policy. "We believe schools should have control over AI in their classrooms," he said. Phillips added that the company sought input from researchers, policymakers, schools and teachers before it changed the controls covering Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search.

### Nvidia, SKT, Rebellions to jointly establish heterogeneous computing system for AI
Source: The Korea Times
https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/business/companies/20261109/nvidia-skt-rebellions-to-jointly-establish-heterogeneous-computing-system-for-ai-1?utm_source=rss

Nvidia, SK Telecom and the chip designer Rebellions plan to jointly build a hybrid artificial intelligence computing system for SK Telecom's data centers, according to the Korea Times. The three companies signed a memorandum of understanding on Monday at AI Day Seoul, a two-day Nvidia event in the south of the city. Under the arrangement, Rebellions' neural processing chips will connect to Nvidia's graphics processors through a program called NVLink Fusion. The program lets major cloud operators and makers of custom chips attach their own AI processors to Nvidia's rack-scale systems. South Korea's science ministry said the deal could create new applications for the country's advanced chips and strengthen the position of Korean companies in overseas markets. Nvidia also held a forum that drew more than 20 Korean startups. The company said it plans to invest in Point2 Technology and to increase its holdings in Motif Technologies, RLWRLD and Upstage.

### SplatAnything: Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-forward 3DGS across Arbitrary Scenes
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264

The researchers behind a paper titled SplatAnything: Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-forward 3DGS across Arbitrary Scenes want a widely used 3D modelling technique to perform more consistently across very different scenes. The technique, known as 3D Gaussian splatting, represents a scene as a large number of small, blob-like shapes called Gaussians. In quicker feed-forward versions, a neural network predicts those shapes in one pass. The researchers found that existing approaches resize scenes using one overall distance measure, yet the spacing between neighbouring Gaussians still swings widely depending on scene size and camera placement. That leaves the network uncertain how big each Gaussian should be. Their solution, called GSNorm, rescales each scene using a robust estimate of that spacing, giving the network a steadier reference point.

### Fal raises $1.3 billion as demand for AI inference grows
Fal, a company that hosts video, image and audio models, has raised $1.3 billion in a Series F round, TechCrunch reported on 3 November. Bessemer Venture Partners led the round, which reportedly values Fal at $16 billion, twice its valuation in April. Fal disclosed its Series E and Series F at the same time, for a combined $1.65 billion. That total includes the $1.3 billion rather than adding to it. The deal points to investors moving money toward the business of running AI models for customers. Even so, the high cost of computing continues to put pressure on the profit margins of these inference providers.

### US Justice Department announces a consolidated AI enforcement task force
The US Justice Department announced on 5 November what it calls a Super Intelligence Task Force. The unit will coordinate enforcement against illegal uses of artificial intelligence by foreign actors, criminal groups and companies. According to Nextgov, it absorbs the department's earlier AI litigation task force and will draw up guidance on prosecutions and compliance. While the public announcement came this week, the memorandum behind it is dated 13 October. The phrase super intelligence reflects the administration's own choice of words. It does not indicate that artificial intelligence surpassing humans across the board has been achieved.

### Google adds two new TPU chips as it takes on Nvidia in AI hardware
Google is rolling out two new versions of its own Tensor Processing Unit chip, Reuters reports. The first, the 8tx, is designed for government agencies and regulated industries, and includes encryption in the hardware itself to safeguard sensitive information. The second, the 8i Max, targets demanding AI workloads such as coding assistants, mathematical reasoning and drug discovery. Google is increasingly selling access to its chips through Google Cloud instead of reserving them for its own operations. The move shows that competition in AI now extends beyond the models themselves. Command of chips, cloud capacity and computing costs is growing just as important, and Google is presenting its processors as an alternative to Nvidia's hardware.


Source: Reuters https://www.reuters.com/business/google-to-introduce-two-variants-its-in-house-chips-bloomberg-news-reports-2026-11-09/

### Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation after reports its CEO was targeted by foreign intelligence
Nvidia said its board of directors has formed an independent committee, backed by outside lawyers, to look into reports that chief executive Jensen Huang may have been the target of a foreign intelligence operation. The company said it has no current reason to think that national security or its own confidential information was compromised. Nvidia added that Huang has agreed to fully cooperate with the inquiry. Because Nvidia plays a central role in the worldwide AI supply chain, questions about the security of its leadership and of access to advanced chips carry weight far beyond the company.

### Peppermint raises ₹100 crore to expand autonomous cleaning robots
Peppermint Robotics, which is based in Pune and Austin, announced on 4 November that it has raised ₹100 crore in a Pre-Series B round. The round was led by pi Ventures and Crane Venture Partners, with Asiana Fund also taking part. The deal combines fresh investment with sales of existing shares, meaning the business itself will not receive the full ₹100 crore. The company's autonomous cleaning robots rely on computer vision and LiDAR to map spaces, plan routes and steer around obstacles. Peppermint plans to increase manufacturing, move into cleanroom robotics and set up an assembly line in North America. Figures on its deployments and revenue growth are the company's own claims.
