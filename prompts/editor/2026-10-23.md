### AI-found PHP flaws push governments to take websites offline
Two critical flaws in PHP, the language behind more than 70% of websites, prompted a rare NSA warning to patch immediately or take sites offline. Germany, France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Finland and the Czech Republic took some government sites down while patching, and Japan's Prime Minister warned about the risk. The flaws, dubbed PHP2Shell, let an attacker run commands on an unpatched server without a password. They were found with the help of AI models: security firm Calif, working with OpenAI, reported the first in August and Wiz reported the second. The PHP Foundation warned that AI tools now find bugs faster "for defenders and attackers alike". (Themes: technology trends; AI policy and regulation)

### Thousands of AI agents read a mouse-brain atlas in 32 minutes
OpenAI, PerturbAI and Scripps Research ran thousands of GPT-Rosalind agents in parallel over a whole-brain mouse CRISPR atlas. Each agent took one gene disruption, studied the cell responses, searched the literature and proposed explanations, and the results were merged into a searchable "reasoning atlas". The team estimates the same work would take people months to a year. The agents reproduced human findings and flagged new leads, including a possible link between the Parkinson-like Perry syndrome gene DCTN1 and cholesterol production. In blinded tests, experts preferred these reports 75% of the time. These are hypotheses to test in the lab, not discoveries, and the results are OpenAI's own account. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

# USA

### Outside evaluators found holes in OpenAI's agent-monitoring system
The nonprofits METR and Redwood Research said on 22 October that they ran a pilot review of OpenAI's internal misalignment-monitoring system. From 21 July to 11 August, one person from each group had chaperoned, on-site access to OpenAI's codebase and staff. They found several "novel vulnerabilities", including an exploit that would let agents keep "hundreds" of recent actions from being monitored. OpenAI says it has patched the flaws that affected production safeguards. Only a high-level summary is public; a 61-page report went to OpenAI. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)

WORLD
IMF and OECD warn that AI could widen the gap between rich and poor countries
Category: AI policy, economic impact and technology adoption
Status: Findings and projections reported by the organisations.

Officials from the International Monetary Fund and the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development have warned that AI could increase global economic inequality unless developing economies, smaller businesses and younger workers gain better access to computing infrastructure, electricity and relevant skills.

The IMF estimates that effective AI adoption could add up to 0.5 percentage points to annual global economic growth over the medium term. The OECD estimates a potential increase of 0.4% to 1.2% in annual productivity growth across its member countries.

However, officials emphasised that actual workplace adoption remains limited compared with consumer use. The IMF also estimates that approximately 40% of jobs worldwide could be exposed to AI. Exposure does not necessarily mean that a job will disappear. Reuters

Why it matters: AI's economic benefits will depend on how effectively organisations integrate the technology into real business processes. Access to AI alone is not enough; businesses also need trained employees, suitable infrastructure and measurable productivity improvements.

For Indian SMEs, the opportunity is to use AI to improve accounting, customer service, inventory management and decision-making without taking on unnecessary technology costs.

Source: Reuters — IMF and OECD warn AI could widen the rich-poor country divide
AI data-centre expansion encounters a physical infrastructure bottleneck
Category: Technology trends and AI infrastructure
Status: Industry assessment.

French cable manufacturer Nexans has warned that global production capacity for the electrical cables required by AI data centres is struggling to keep pace with demand.

Data centres need extensive low- and medium-voltage cabling to connect power infrastructure. According to Nexans CFO Vincent Piquet, establishing a new production line for certain specialised cables can take approximately three years.

Power constraints have already delayed some projects. The report also highlights how developers are competing for electrical capacity and how some utilities are moving towards longer-term supply agreements. Reuters

Why it matters: The AI race depends on more than chips and software. Electricity connections, cables, cooling systems and data-centre construction are becoming important constraints on how quickly computing capacity can expand.

For investors and business leaders, this means infrastructure availability and operating costs deserve as much attention as model performance.

Meta's rising AI spending raises questions about returns on investment
Category: AI industry, financial performance and investment
Status: Confirmed financial results and management plans.

Meta Platforms reported stronger-than-expected quarterly revenue and highlighted growing adoption of its Muse AI agent. Nevertheless, its shares fell more than 5% in early trading on 22 October as investors weighed the costs of its AI expansion.

Meta expects 2026 capital expenditure of between $135 billion and $145 billion. The company also reported negative free cash flow of $2.12 billion in the third quarter.

Alongside its AI investment programme, Meta announced plans to introduce a new class of non-voting shares, providing additional flexibility for acquisitions and employee compensation while preserving existing voting control. Reuters

Why it matters: The announcement illustrates a central question facing the AI industry: how quickly can enormous infrastructure investments translate into sustainable revenue and cash flow?

For CFOs, the lesson is straightforward. AI investments should be evaluated through productivity gains, incremental revenue, operating costs and cash-flow impact—not simply the number of tools deployed.

South Korea activates emergency cybersecurity measures following critical PHP vulnerabilities
Category: AI-related technology trends and cybersecurity
Status: Confirmed government response to software vulnerabilities.

South Korea has raised its private-sector cybersecurity alert level and activated emergency response measures following the discovery of critical vulnerabilities in PHP, a widely used programming language for websites and web applications.

The government convened an emergency meeting involving intelligence, defence and science ministry officials. Authorities also issued security guidance to approximately 28,000 companies with registered chief information security officers, alongside critical infrastructure operators, hosting firms and data centres.

The vulnerabilities could permit unauthorised attackers to execute malicious commands, steal information or take websites offline. PHP developers issued emergency fixes on Wednesday, and South Korean authorities urged affected organisations to install the updates promptly. Reuters

Why it matters: The incident demonstrates how weaknesses in widely deployed software can become systemic risks. As AI tools accelerate software development and security research, organisations must ensure that vulnerability discovery is matched by timely patching and coordinated incident response.

Businesses should review their PHP versions, apply relevant security updates and confirm that internet-facing systems are adequately protected.

Closing perspective
The AI industry is increasingly being shaped by three forces: investment, infrastructure and implementation.

For Indian enterprises, the opportunity is substantial—but adoption must be accompanied by sound financial discipline, strong cybersecurity and the skills needed to make AI work in practice.

The central question for business leaders is no longer simply how to adopt AI, but how to make AI adoption productive, secure and financially sustainable.
