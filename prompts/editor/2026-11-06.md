# USA

### OpenAI launches GPT-6 Cyber and rates it "Critical" for cyber risk
OpenAI has released GPT-6 Cyber, a model trained to find and validate exploitable software flaws while staying inside the scope a security researcher sets. OpenAI rates the model's cyber capability as "Critical" under its own safety framework, so for now only selected customers in its Daybreak Red programme can use it, through the API and Codex, with wider access planned in the coming weeks. OpenAI says the model found 81 previously unknown high-severity flaws in Firefox, and scored 48.5 percent on its ExploitGym test, up from 32.1 percent for GPT-5.6 Cyber. These are OpenAI's own figures.

Why it matters: AI that can find serious software flaws is valuable to defenders and dangerous in the wrong hands. Releasing it only to vetted security teams is a test of whether controlled access can keep that balance.

### METR finds AI is helping Anthropic improve its models about twice as fast
The independent evaluator METR has published an assessment of Anthropic's internal research from August to October. It estimates that Anthropic's model capabilities are improving about 1.9 times faster than they did between March 2024 and October 2025, and says AI tools that speed up staff work on coding and experiments probably account for most of that. METR found no clear bottleneck that would stop further speed-up within a year. Anthropic redacted much of the evidence METR considered most informative, citing competitive confidentiality, so the full basis cannot be checked publicly.

Why it matters: If AI is now speeding up the building of the next AI, development could accelerate quickly, which makes independent measurement and transparency more important.

# WORLD

### NATO opens a counter-drone marketplace as Shield AI agrees to buy Echodyne
NATO's communications agency has opened a marketplace that lets allies order from 16 preselected counter-drone systems without running their own tenders, with 3.1 million euros set aside for further testing. France's Alta Ares, which builds AI-powered interceptors, says it won six of the 16 lots. Separately, Shield AI, maker of the Hivemind AI pilot, has agreed to buy the radar maker Echodyne to strengthen its counter-drone systems.

Why it matters: Drones have made fast, AI-assisted air defence a priority for militaries, and NATO is now buying it the way governments buy software: from a ready-made catalogue.

# CHINA

### China cracks down on AI fakes that impersonate celebrities
China's internet regulator, the Cyberspace Administration of China, says it has removed more than 47,000 posts and penalised 3,961 accounts that used AI to impersonate celebrities. The campaign targets AI-generated faces and voices used to mislead audiences or sell products.

Why it matters: Cheap, convincing deepfakes are turning celebrity impersonation into a scale problem, and regulators are starting to act against the accounts that spread them, not just the content.

# INDIA

### India's CERT-In asks organisations to audit what their AI agents can access and do
India's national cybersecurity agency, CERT-In, has called on organisations to test whether their AI agents can expose confidential information or carry out actions they were never authorised to take. Its AI security auditing guidelines ask for tight permission controls, a record of every action an agent takes, a way to interrupt an agent in an emergency, and human approval before any consequential step. The guidelines also cover prompt injection, where malicious instructions hidden in documents, emails or web pages try to take control of an agent.

Why it matters: Indian businesses are starting to give AI agents access to email, documents, internal systems and financial records. CERT-In's guidance makes agent permissions and audit trails a basic security control, not an afterthought.
