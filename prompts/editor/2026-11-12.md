# WORLD

### Rights groups urge a treaty on "killer robots" before next week's UN talks
Amnesty International, Human Rights Watch and the Stop Killer Robots campaign are urging governments to open time-bound negotiations on a legally binding protocol on autonomous weapons at the Convention on Conventional Weapons Review Conference in Geneva, which runs from 16 to 20 November. The review conference takes place only once every five years, so the groups describe it as a rare chance to act. They warn that weapons systems able to select and attack targets without meaningful human control are already being tested and deployed. Many states back a binding treaty, but major military powers have so far preferred non-binding guidelines, and talks under the convention have stalled for more than a decade.
Source: Human Rights Watch https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/11/12/global-treaty-needed-to-combat-autonomous-weapons-threat

# USA

### Google launches the Gemini Decisions API for fast yes/no and scoring calls
Google has introduced Gemini Decision 1 and a new Decisions API. Instead of writing a long answer, the model evaluates up to 1,000 closed questions about a single input in one pass: yes or no, multiple choice, or scoring against a rubric. The input can be text, images, audio, video or PDFs. Google says it returns probabilities and confidence scores in 150 to 250 milliseconds, at ten cents per million input tokens, with no charge for output. It is aimed at routing requests, checking policies and grading work inside AI agents, where a quick decision matters more than an essay. The accuracy claims come from Google.
Source: Google https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/

### DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 predicts how DNA changes affect individual cell types
Google DeepMind has announced AlphaGenome 2, a model that predicts how variations in DNA affect different human cell types. It was trained on more than 100 million single-cell profiles from 68 tissues, and DeepMind says it identifies nearly twice as many variants that change gene expression in specific cell types as its previous model. The same genetic variant can behave differently depending on the cell it occurs in, so cell-level predictions could help researchers trace how diseases arise. The model is available for non-commercial research through the AlphaGenome API, with a private preview for commercial research. It is a research tool and is not approved for clinical diagnosis.
Source: Google DeepMind https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/

### Databricks launches an always-on Genie agent for enterprise work
Databricks has introduced a new generation of Genie, its AI agent, built to work continuously on complex business tasks rather than only answer employees' questions. It can monitor data, carry out longer-running assignments and bring findings to people without being asked. A business ontology gives it context about a company's data, people and processes. Databricks also describes controls for administrators, including limits on which tools the agent can use, traces of every action it takes and caps on spending. The launch reflects a wider shift from AI assistants that respond to prompts toward agents that act as operational coworkers, with governance built in from the start.
Source: Databricks https://www.databricks.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/databricks-launches-new-always-genie-autonomous-ai-complex

### United Airlines restricts third-party AI agents from booking flights
United Airlines says it is restricting third-party AI agents from using its website and app to make bookings and other changes on behalf of customers. The airline cites risks to account credentials, unintended purchases, changes to reservations and loyalty points. It also points to regulations that require a human to make certain acknowledgements, such as confirming baggage restrictions at check-in. The move marks a real-world boundary for agentic AI: an agent may be able to book a flight, but that does not mean a business will let it transact for a customer. Companies will need clear rules on identity, authorisation, liability and human approval before agents can shop on people's behalf.
Source: United Airlines https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496

### Google releases A2UI 1.0, an open standard for AI-generated screens
Google has released version 1.0 of A2UI, an open protocol that lets AI agents return interactive screens instead of only text. An agent describes a form, a chart or another interface, and the app draws it with its own native components, so the result looks and behaves like the rest of the app. The release adds function calls in both directions and renderers for web and mobile frameworks. Instead of reading a block of text and then doing the next step by hand, users could get a screen to review, edit and confirm. The standard is open, so other companies' agents and apps can adopt it too.
Source: Google Developers Blog https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-10-build-delightful-fast-reliable-agentic-experiences/

# CHINA

### Baidu launches ERNIE Lightning and a video model at Baidu World 2026
At its Baidu World 2026 conference, Baidu introduced ERNIE Lightning, a reasoning model built first for Baidu Search, and ERNIE-Wizshot-ME, which turns a still photo into a talking video with expressive faces, priced at 0.7 yuan per second of video. Both are available to business customers on Baidu's Qianfan cloud platform. Baidu says Lightning cuts the cost of its inference engine by more than 30 percent when running on chips from Kunlunxin, its own chip unit. That is a company claim, and Baidu has not published benchmarks. The launch underlines Baidu's push to run its AI on home-grown chips as US export controls limit access to Nvidia hardware.
Source: TPS Report https://tpsreport.news/news/baidu-ernie-lightning-wizshot-me-qianfan

### Beijing plans capacity for 200,000 robots a year by 2028
Beijing's industry bureau has issued an embodied-AI action plan for 2026 to 2028. It aims for at least five world-class humanoid robot makers, at least 200 robot-related companies, capacity to build 200,000 robots a year, and a training cluster of 50,000 petaflops for robot AI models. The plan is dated 11 November. China is betting heavily on humanoid and service robots as a new industry, and city governments are competing to host the leading makers. Beijing already hosts several humanoid start-ups and staged the first humanoid robot games, and the plan is meant to turn prototypes into mass production.
Source: EastFrontier https://eastfrontier.com/2026/11/12/beijings-embodied-ai-plan-targets-200-000-robots-a-year-and-200-firms/

# ASIA

### Japan weighs binding orders and penalties for AI firms after incidents
Japan is considering legally binding orders, backed by penalties, against AI companies that do not cooperate with the government after incidents involving advanced AI, Kyodo News reports, citing a source. A government working group is reviewing the country's 2025 AI law, which relies on voluntary cooperation and has no penalties at all. An outline of the review is due by the end of the year. The move would mark a shift for Japan, which has so far taken one of the lightest approaches to AI regulation among major economies, and it follows growing concern over misuse of powerful models and cyber incidents linked to AI.
Source: Kyodo News https://english.kyodonews.net/articles/-/91104

# INDIA

### OpenAI now processes API requests inside India
OpenAI has launched local processing for its API in India. For supported workloads, both the request and the model's computation now happen inside the country, not just the storage of data. The service starts with GPT-6 Luna through the Responses and Chat Completions APIs, and supported customer content is also stored in India. It is aimed at businesses with data-localisation requirements, such as banks and other financial firms. For regulated organisations, where data is processed can matter as much as where it is stored, so the change could remove a major obstacle to using AI in sensitive business workflows.
Source: The Week https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2026/11/12/openai-to-process-ai-requests-within-india-for-businesses-with-data-localisation-needs.html
