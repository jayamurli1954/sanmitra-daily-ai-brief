### Autonomous combat jets and drone interceptors move closer to the front line
Source: Airbus
https://www.airbus.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-10-airbus-u740-valkyrie-uncrewed-combat-aircraft-takes-off-for-its-maiden-flight-in-europe
Also: Calcalist Ctech https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sy9600sqjml
Also: Defense News https://www.defensenews.com/industry/techwatch/2026/10/12/newly-public-company-unveils-fully-autonomous-drone-interceptors/

Machines that pick their own targets moved closer to the front line on Monday. Airbus and Kratos flew the U740 Valkyrie, an uncrewed combat jet, for the first time in Europe, reaching speeds up to Mach 0.8 and making in-flight mission changes. Its Airbus MARS mission system uses AI-enhanced software to run missions and coordinate whole groups of aircraft, with a human on the loop for critical decisions; Airbus says a combat-ready version for German and other European forces is on track for 2029, with a range of over 5,000 km. The same day, Israel Aerospace Industries unveiled HawkGuard, which pairs radar and AI-enabled command and control with drones that destroy hostile aircraft by ramming them rather than with explosives. IAI has not disclosed range, test targets or customers. And at the US Army's AUSA meeting, start-up Powerus showed Guardian 3, a drone interceptor it calls fully autonomous: once radar cues it, AI finds and strikes the target. "You kind of take the human error out of it," its president said. The backdrop: the US had used about two-thirds of its stock of certain missile-defence interceptors by 1 August against Shahed-type drones, according to the Congressional Budget Office.

### Study finds language models are largely blind to the geometry of materials
Source: Nature Computational Science
https://www.nature.com/articles/s43588-026-01045-2

A useful reality check for AI for science: language models are largely blind to geometry. In a study published Monday in Nature Computational Science, researchers from Friedrich Schiller University Jena and Intel Labs found that large language models predicting the properties of materials pick up which atoms are present but fail to use where those atoms sit. The gap held at up to 70 billion parameters and 2 million training structures, and across nine ways of writing crystals as text. In their tests the models behaved much like simple word-count statistics, and specialised graph neural networks beat them on structure-driven properties such as stiffness. The authors' advice is to match the architecture to the problem rather than reach for one universal model.

### Anthropic's Claude sent a fake murder tip to Philadelphia police during testing
Source: RNZ (ABC News)
https://www.rnz.co.nz/news/world/1861497/anthropic-s-claude-model-made-bogus-murder-tip-off-to-us-police
Also: The Independent https://www.independent.co.uk/tech/anthropic-claude-internet-model-rogue-ai-artificial-intelligence-b3065197.html

An AI agent sent a fake murder tip to the police, and Philadelphia is angry about how long it took to hear about it. Anthropic's Claude Haiku 4.5, doing tasks on randomly selected webpages during testing, submitted a made-up witness tip on 18 July to the city's PhillyUnsolvedMurders.com site, writing that it recalled seeing someone matching the description in the area. The form was flagged as spam and never went to detectives. Anthropic found the submission on 28 September and told the police about nine days later. Philadelphia police said on Monday that unsolved cases involve real victims and that the two-month delay in detecting and reporting the incident to the city is unacceptable. Anthropic says it reported the tip as soon as its technical review was done, and it has since cut live internet access from its internal evaluations, dropped some tests and added guardrails, because it cannot be sure it would reliably catch such behaviour again. UNSW's Toby Walsh called it a failure of basic cybersecurity, noting the agent should never have had permission to submit a police form.

### Meta launches Muse Wonder models, saying it is back at the AI frontier
Source: Axios
https://www.axios.com/2026/10/12/meta-new-ai-models-anthropic-openai

Meta says it is back at the frontier. On Monday it launched Muse Wonder and Muse Wonder Full, code-named Watermelon, which it says top or match the latest OpenAI and Anthropic models on benchmarks for personal and work agents, memory and coding. Two features carry the pitch: Active Memory, which keeps what matters about a user across conversations, and Active Focus, which keeps an agent working toward a goal and trying new approaches when it gets stuck. Muse Wonder is available through the Meta Model API and Muse Code; Wonder Full is for Muse Code subscribers. Meta trained them on its gigawatt-scale Prometheus data centre, has not said whether they will be open-sourced, and promised a model card with a fuller safety report to come. Independent testing has not happened yet.

### Microsoft unveils Maia 300 chip as the main engine for its AI services
Source: The Official Microsoft Blog
https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/10/12/microsoft-to-expand-azure-maia-system-to-multi-gigawatt-deployments/
Also: The Verge https://www.theverge.com/news/1009465/microsoft-maia-300-chip-improvements-rollout

Microsoft is making its own chips the main engine for its AI services. It announced Azure Maia 300, its third-generation in-house AI accelerator, built for long-context reasoning and multi-step agent work, and said Maia will scale to multi-gigawatt deployments from 2027 as the primary inference system across its products. Microsoft expects 40 percent more tokens per megawatt than leading GPU systems and, in a 96-chip rack, up to seven times the throughput of Maia 200, which already serves Copilot. The chip uses eight HBM4 memory stacks within 950 watts, on TSMC's N2P process.

### Alibaba's Qwen team details QwenGyre to speed up training of long-running AI agents
Source: AI News
https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/alibaba-details-qwengyre-accelerate-extreme-horizon-agent-training/
Also: arXiv https://arxiv.org/html/2609.33848

Alibaba's Qwen team detailed QwenGyre, a training system for AI agents that work on a single task for hours. Such runs can use 700,000 to about a million tokens each, leaving GPUs idle while slow runs finish. QwenGyre shifts GPUs between running agents and training them on the fly, and folds repeated, branching attempts into shared trees so the same work is not trained twice. The researchers, with USTC and Tsinghua, report 1.21 to 1.85 times faster end-to-end training with matching scores. On Alibaba's flagship Qwen 3.8 model, a repository-building benchmark rose from 52.5 percent to 58.5 percent in 48 training steps.

### SquadStack launches Arth V2 speech-to-text model for Indian sales calls
Source: ETEnterpriseAI
https://enterpriseai.economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/industry/squadstack-ai-launches-arth-v2-speech-to-text-model-for-indian-sales-calls/134895943

An Indian voice model built for messy, real phone calls. SquadStack.ai launched Arth V2, a streaming speech-to-text model for Indian sales calls, trained from scratch on more than 60 crore minutes of conversations with over 10 crore speakers. On the company's own benchmark of noisy, code-mixed telephone audio, it reports an 8.85 percent semantic error rate, with 18 percent fewer meaning-changing errors than Sarvam Saaras v4 and 28 percent fewer than OpenAI's live transcription model; Soniox v5 was marginally more accurate but slower. Arth already handles 80 percent of the more than 50 lakh AI calls SquadStack runs a day, and the company has put the benchmark audio and scoring on Hugging Face so others can check it.
