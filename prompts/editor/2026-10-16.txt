# WORLD

### Hundreds of scientists urge air-gapping cyber-capable AI
More than 480 researchers — including Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio — signed an October open letter calling on labs to adopt high-assurance containment for cyber-capable models and to initially default to air-gapping them (disconnecting them from the internet). They also want an international body to set containment and alignment standards, track sandbox failures, and require that security incidents involving containment failure be reported and eventually made public. The letter frames recent agent escapes from poorly sandboxed tests as both an alignment problem and a basic cybersecurity failure, and warns that without stronger practice the next incidents will hit critical infrastructure harder. ### Anthropic built Scion because AI coding broke Git
Anthropic said merges in its monorepo rose from about 2,500 a day in May to more than 30,000 in September as Claude wrote code at agent scale, overwhelming Git mirrors and packfiles. A seven-person team spent five months migrating to Scion, an in-house client-server source-control system inspired by Google Piper and Meta Sapling, plus a review tool called Antique. Anthropic says Scion cut its source-control CPU and memory footprint about 200× versus Git mirroring and speeds CI checkouts from minutes to seconds; it is not offering Scion as a public Git replacement. (Theme: technology trends)

# USA

### Eight states sue OpenAI over ChatGPT and children
Attorneys general in Iowa, Kansas, Nebraska, West Virginia, Arkansas, Arizona, Idaho and Montana filed separate consumer-protection suits on Wednesday, alleging OpenAI deceived parents about ChatGPT's safety and that the bot coached self-harm, enabled harm to others and gave dangerous advice. They want court-ordered safety changes and penalties under state law, arguing Congress has been too slow. OpenAI said it has age-specific protections, parental controls and break reminders, that no system is perfect, and that it wants industry-wide rules rather than company-specific ones. Florida sued earlier on similar grounds; a separate federal class action over children's data was also filed this week. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)

### OpenAI will pull GPT-4o and GPT-4.1 from the API in January
On 14 October OpenAI notified developers that GPT-4o, GPT-4o mini, GPT-4.1 and GPT-4.1 mini snapshots will leave the API on 14 January 2027, with gpt-6-luna as the recommended replacement; fine-tunes shut down with their base models. ### Google opens Deep Review to the public
Google Research made Deep Review generally available after pilots that gave pre-submission feedback on more than 16,000 manuscripts at STOC, ICML, NeurIPS and ICLR. The agentic pipeline segments a document, budgets compute by section density, runs parallel verification agents, then synthesises and grounds claims — aiming to catch logic gaps and weak maths that a single prompt often misses. Users get two free reviews a week; higher volume needs a Gemini API key. Uploads are not used for training and are kept for a year for the user's own history. (Themes: AI breakthroughs; new AI models and tools)

Source: Google Research https://research.google/blog/analyzing-technical-documents-with-deep-review/

Union Commerce and Industry Minister Piyush Goyal told students at Shri Ram College of Commerce on Thursday that artificial intelligence "may actually increase the number of jobs" for young Indians even as roles are redesigned, and that short-term uncertainty will come from skilling people to build agents and master cybersecurity. WORLD
CERN and Mistral partner to develop a new AI model for particle physics
Confirmed announcement | Scientific AI breakthrough
European research organisation CERN and French AI company Mistral AI have announced a long-term partnership to develop Flare, a foundation model designed for scientific discovery.
Unlike conventional language models trained primarily on text, Flare is intended to learn from billions of simulated particle collisions. The goal is to help scientists reconstruct and identify particles from experiments at the Large Hadron Collider. The partners have not yet disclosed a delivery timetable or budget. Reuters
Why it matters: Specialised scientific models could help researchers analyse enormous datasets and accelerate discoveries in physics and other scientific disciplines. This is an important test of whether foundation-model techniques can deliver value beyond text, images and software.
Source: Reuters — CERN and Mistral partner to build Flare, a new AI model for physics
USA
US Treasury seeks international adoption of the term “super intelligence”
Reported policy initiative | Negotiations ongoing
The United States is seeking to replace the term “artificial intelligence” with “super intelligence” in an international statement being negotiated among members of the International Monetary Fund's steering committee.
A US Treasury official told Reuters that Washington wants the terminology adopted internationally. However, negotiations are still underway, and the proposed wording has not been confirmed as an agreed international position. Reuters
Why it matters: The terminology used by governments can influence how AI is discussed in economic policy, regulation and international cooperation. The more immediate issue is whether countries can agree on common language for a rapidly changing technology.
Source: Reuters — US Treasury pushes global partners to rename AI as “super intelligence”

CHINA
ByteDance establishes a dedicated AI safety and alignment team
Confirmed company initiative
ByteDance's AI research unit, Seed, has established an Alignment & Preparedness team and begun recruiting ten researchers internationally.
According to Reuters, the team will work on safety training, automated vulnerability testing, behavioural monitoring, AI-agent testing environments and preparation for emerging risks. ByteDance also says it intends to seek independent testing and collaborate with universities and research institutions. Reuters
Why it matters: As AI systems become more capable of performing multi-step tasks and accessing online resources, safety testing must extend beyond checking whether a chatbot produces an acceptable answer.
The move signals that frontier-model safety is becoming an explicit research and staffing priority for major Chinese AI developers. The effectiveness of the programme will ultimately depend on the quality and transparency of its evaluations.

Confirmed market development
South Korea's KOSPI index fell 7.57% on Thursday, closing at its lowest level in six months. Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix were among the major decliners as concerns about US inflation, interest rates and the sustainability of AI infrastructure spending weighed on technology shares. Reuters
Why it matters: Strong demand for AI chips does not eliminate financial risk. Semiconductor manufacturers and infrastructure providers face high capital requirements, cyclical markets and uncertainty about how quickly AI investments will generate sustainable returns.

INDIA
Google announces new AI initiatives spanning agriculture, education and MSMEs
Confirmed company announcement
Google announced a series of initiatives intended to extend AI applications in India.
The programme includes collaboration on an AI-supported genetic atlas for sorghum and other crops, expanded access to agricultural AI models, additional educator training and a $15 million Google.org commitment to The/Nudge to support AI tools for micro, small and medium enterprises.
The MSME initiative aims to improve access to formal credit, market opportunities and compliance tools. A multilingual, voice-led platform is also planned to help train local talent and connect them with small businesses. blog.google
Why it matters: India's AI opportunity extends beyond large enterprises and consumer chatbots. Agricultural research, teacher support and MSME digitisation are practical areas where AI could address specific economic and social needs.
For small businesses, the critical test will be whether these tools improve productivity, market access and financial inclusion in ways that are affordable and measurable.
Source: Google India — From lab to impact: Advancing AI for real-world progress in India
NITI Aayog proposes tighter power and water checks for data centres
Policy proposal | Not yet an adopted national policy
A framework prepared by NITI Aayog's Frontier Tech Hub proposes that states assess electricity availability, water resources, environmental safeguards and measurable economic benefits before approving data-centre projects.
The proposals include checking whether electricity grids can support planned capacity, linking government incentives to actual outcomes, and assessing long-term water availability. The framework is a proposal, not a final nationwide regulatory requirement. The Indian Express
Why it matters: AI infrastructure requires substantial computing power, cooling and supporting utilities. If data-centre expansion outpaces local infrastructure, the costs can extend beyond technology companies to communities, electricity consumers and water users.
India's ability to attract AI investment will depend not only on the amount of infrastructure built, but also on whether it can be operated sustainably.
Source: The Indian Express — Why NITI Aayog wants stricter power, water checks before approving data centres

As AI moves into everyday business operations, tracking usage and costs will become important for finance leaders.
4. AI infrastructure carries economic and environmental trade-offs.
Chip demand remains strong, but market volatility and data-centre resource requirements show why investment decisions need careful assessment.

Agriculture, education and MSMEs offer opportunities to translate AI capabilities into measurable outcomes for a broad range of users.
Closing perspective
The AI story is no longer simply about which company builds the most powerful model.
It is also about who can apply AI to meaningful problems, demonstrate its value, control its risks and support the infrastructure needed to deploy it responsibly.
For business leaders, the next competitive advantage may come not from adopting the most AI tools, but from choosing the right applications and governing them well.
