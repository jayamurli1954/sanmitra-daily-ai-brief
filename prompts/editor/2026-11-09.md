# WORLD

### 42AI emerges with a model designed to predict human behavior
On 2 November, TechCrunch reported the public debut of Israeli-American startup 42AI, which says its “behavioral world model” estimates how people will respond to unfamiliar situations, initially for financial brokerages. Its disclosed $62.5 million seed funding came from founders and Infinity Group over three years—not a new one-day fundraising haul. Its predictive performance remains a company claim; its much-cited “98% accuracy” measures trading flows falling within a range, not correctly predicting every trade. Individual profiling also raises privacy and conflict-of-interest questions. (Theme: New AI models and tools)

### Fal raises $1.3 billion as demand for AI inference grows
TechCrunch reported on 3 November that Fal, which hosts video, image and audio models, raised a $1.3 billion Series F led by Bessemer Venture Partners at a reported $16 billion valuation—double April’s valuation. Fal announced its Series E and F together, totaling $1.65 billion; those are not two additional sums to add to the $1.3 billion. The financing highlights investment shifting toward serving models, although expensive computing continues to squeeze inference providers’ margins. (Theme: AI industry and startup news)

### US Justice Department announces a consolidated AI enforcement task force
On 5 November, the Justice Department publicly announced its “Super Intelligence Task Force,” coordinating enforcement against unlawful AI use by foreign actors, criminal networks and companies. Nextgov reports that it incorporates the earlier AI litigation task force and will develop prosecution and compliance guidance. The announcement is this week’s news, but the underlying memorandum is dated 13 October. “Super intelligence” is the administration’s terminology here, not evidence that superhuman general intelligence has been achieved. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)

### Google expands Singapore research with more than $6 million in funding
Google announced on 3 November that, alongside its newly established Singapore research hub, it is expanding academic partnerships with more than $6 million in research and capacity-building support. Projects include AI-assisted Alzheimer’s research at NUS, computing efficiency and regional languages, AI safety studies at NTU, and support for INSEAD’s Thrive Lab. These are funding commitments and research plans—not completed scientific breakthroughs. (Theme: Technology trends)

### Peppermint raises ₹100 crore to expand autonomous cleaning robots
Pune- and Austin-based Peppermint Robotics announced a ₹100 crore Pre-Series B on 4 November, led by pi Ventures and Crane Venture Partners, with Asiana Fund participating. The transaction includes both primary funding and secondary share sales, so not all ₹100 crore goes into the business. Its robots use computer vision and LiDAR for mapping, route planning and obstacle avoidance. Plans include more manufacturing, cleanroom robotics and a North American assembly line; deployment and revenue-growth figures are company claims. (Theme: AI industry and startup news)

# USA

### Google adds two new TPU chips as it takes on Nvidia in AI hardware
Google is introducing two new variants of its in-house Tensor Processing Unit. The 8tx is built for government agencies and regulated industries, with encryption built into the hardware to protect sensitive data. The 8i Max is aimed at compute-heavy AI work such as coding assistants, mathematical reasoning and drug discovery. Google is increasingly offering its own chips to customers through Google Cloud, rather than keeping them for internal use.

Why it matters: The AI race is no longer only about models. Control over chips, cloud capacity and computing costs is becoming just as important, and Google is positioning its TPUs as an alternative to Nvidia's hardware.

Source: Reuters https://www.reuters.com/business/google-to-introduce-two-variants-its-in-house-chips-bloomberg-news-reports-2026-11-09/

### Nvidia's board opens an independent investigation after reports its CEO was targeted by foreign intelligence
Nvidia's board of directors has set up an independent committee, supported by outside legal counsel, to investigate reports that chief executive Jensen Huang may have been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. Nvidia says it currently has no reason to believe that national security or company secrets were compromised, and that Huang has agreed to cooperate fully.

Why it matters: Nvidia sits at the centre of the global AI supply chain. Security questions around its leadership and access to advanced chips matter well beyond the company itself.

Source: Nvidia https://investor.nvidia.com/news/press-release-details/2026/Statement-from-NVIDIA-Board-of-Directors/default.aspx
