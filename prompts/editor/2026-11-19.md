### **1. Microsoft Agent Framework hit by a supply-chain attack**

Security researchers at Wiz uncovered two malicious releases, versions 1.26.1 and 1.26.2, of Microsoft's Python `agent-framework` package on PyPI. The packages contained credential-stealing code capable of collecting Azure, GitHub, cloud and development-environment credentials. Both releases were removed, but organisations that installed them should investigate and rotate potentially exposed credentials.

**Why it matters:** AI development frameworks are becoming part of the software supply chain. Compromising an agent framework can expose far more than the application using it.

### **11. Sarvam's Token Factory triggers debate over India's sovereign AI ambitions**

Bengaluru-based Sarvam AI has faced questions after its Token Factory inference platform began offering foreign open-weight models, including Chinese models such as DeepSeek, Qwen, Moonshot and GLM, alongside its own models.

Sarvam has defended the decision, saying the platform operates on privately procured GPUs located in India, not government-subsidised infrastructure. The company says its earlier subsidised GPU access under the IndiaAI Mission ended in March 2026.

The debate centres on an important distinction: does sovereign AI mean developing every model domestically, or can foreign models run on Indian infrastructure while keeping inference workloads in India?

**Why it matters:** India's AI strategy must balance indigenous model development, affordable access to global technology, data sovereignty and commercial viability. This is a particularly relevant discussion for Indian enterprises evaluating where and how their AI workloads are processed.
