# WORLD

### Claude writes machine-checked proofs for 123 open-source libraries
Anthropic and the verification firm Theorem used Claude to produce formal, machine-checked proofs for 239 fuzzing entry points across 123 widely used open-source libraries, working at the level of compiled RISC-V code. The proofs show those entry points will not crash on inputs under 64 MB, under set assumptions such as single-threaded static binaries and modelled Linux behaviour, and the effort turned up bugs that had gone unnoticed for years. Anthropic says it does not prove the libraries are bug-free or correct on everyday x86 or ARM deployments. This is Anthropic's own account. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

Source: Anthropic https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification

### Microsoft's diagnostic AI beats generalist doctors on hard cases, per Science
A Science paper on Microsoft's MAI-DxO, a system of AI agents that asks questions, orders tests and debates a diagnosis step by step, reports 83.2% accuracy on complex New England Journal of Medicine cases versus 36.1% for individual generalist physicians. It was a text-only benchmark, so it says nothing yet about specialists, team care, imaging or real patient outcomes. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

## 🌍 WORLD — Cybersecurity and AI Safety

### 1. AI-Discovered Software Flaws Could Trigger More Cybersecurity Emergencies

Cybersecurity experts interviewed by Reuters warn that AI is accelerating the discovery of software vulnerabilities, leaving organisations less time to patch weaknesses before attackers attempt to exploit them.

Recent PHP vulnerabilities have forced government agencies and businesses to take precautionary measures, including temporarily taking websites offline. Experts disagree on whether the increase in discovered flaws will necessarily translate into more successful attacks, but the speed of discovery is changing the cybersecurity landscape.

**Why it matters:** Organisations need faster vulnerability assessment, patching and incident response. AI can strengthen cyber defence, but it can also reduce the time available to respond to threats.

🔗 **Source:** Reuters  
https://www.reuters.com/legal/litigation/ai-discovered-bugs-may-soon-trigger-fresh-disruptions-cyber-experts-say-2026-10-23/

### 2. OpenAI and Poland's NASK Join Forces on AI Safety Research

OpenAI and Poland's NASK National Research Institute have signed an agreement to explore collaboration on AI safety, security and technical research.

The partnership will focus on evaluating increasingly capable AI systems, identifying potential vulnerabilities and strengthening safeguards. Specific projects and timelines have not yet been announced.

**Why it matters:** As AI agents become more capable, independent research partnerships can help identify risks, improve testing and strengthen confidence in how these systems are deployed.

## 🇺🇸 USA — AI Accountability and Legal Oversight

### 3. US Appeals Court Reassigns Case After AI-Related Legal Errors

A US federal appeals court overturned a ruling blocking Mississippi's restrictions on diversity, equity and inclusion programmes in public educational institutions and reassigned the case to another judge after errors involving AI-generated or AI-assisted legal references.

The original judge had acknowledged that a court staff member used Perplexity without properly verifying the material. The appeals court criticised unsupported factual claims, legal errors and references to nonexistent authorities.

**Why it matters:** AI can assist legal research, but unverified citations can undermine judicial decisions and public confidence. Professional accountability and human verification remain essential wherever legal rights are at stake.

🔗 **Source:** Reuters  
https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/appeals-court-lifts-block-mississippis-dei-ban-ejects-judge-over-ai-errors-2026-10-22/

## 🇨🇳 CHINA — AI Policy, Employment and Global Expansion

### 4. China Urges Government Agencies to Address AI-Related Job Displacement

China's State Council has called on government agencies to take effective measures to mitigate job displacement associated with artificial intelligence, according to Reuters reporting on new employment guidelines issued on 23 October.

The guidance reflects growing recognition that AI adoption can reshape labour markets and create pressure on workers whose tasks are automated. The report does not specify all the measures or implementation timelines.

**Why it matters:** AI policy is increasingly about more than technological leadership. Governments must also consider workforce transitions, reskilling and how the benefits of automation are distributed.

Chinese AI developers DeepSeek and MiniMax have reportedly completed registration procedures allowing eligible models to be open-sourced and licensed for overseas use, according to reporting based on a statement from a state-media-affiliated account.

**Why it matters:** China is attempting to balance strategic control over sensitive AI technologies with the international expansion of its domestic AI industry. The outcome could influence competition in open models and global AI services.

## 🌏 ASIA — AI-Powered Cyber Defence

### 6. Japan Plans AI-Powered Cyberattack Simulations After PHP Vulnerabilities

Japan is planning a team that will use AI to simulate cyberattacks and identify weaknesses in technology systems following the discovery of serious PHP vulnerabilities.

The flaws have prompted organisations in multiple countries to strengthen defences, while some services have been temporarily suspended during security measures. Japan's proposed approach aims to anticipate attacker methods rather than relying exclusively on reactive responses.

**Why it matters:** AI-assisted security testing could help organisations identify weaknesses before criminals exploit them. The challenge is to deploy these tools in controlled environments and ensure that defensive capabilities do not create new risks.

### 7. Goa Unveils AI Policy and Plans a ₹250 Crore Innovation Push

Goa has unveiled its AI Policy 2026 at the Goa AI Tech Conclave, alongside initiatives to develop an AI Centre of Excellence with BITS Pilani Goa and strengthen the state's technology ecosystem.

The state plans to invest ₹250 crore over five years through an innovation fund, with a focus on skills, startups, applied research and responsible AI adoption. It also aims to develop 50,000 AI-ready professionals by 2030 and explore a Konkani large language model.
