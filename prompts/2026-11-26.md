### Dozens of court cases in England and Wales affected by undisclosed AI use in 2026
Lead: yes
Source: The Guardian
https://is.gd/hA90Pw

More than two dozen court cases in England and Wales have been affected this year by artificial intelligence use that went undisclosed, according to an investigation reported by the Guardian. Judges have objected to witness statements produced by AI, to case law that does not exist and to invented legislation. Those found misusing the tools in 2026 include lawyers, police officers and claimants. In one high court fight between tax lawyers, the judge condemned the "unacceptable" reliance on generative AI hallucinations, along with citations to cases that were never decided. In a grievous bodily harm case, a police officer turned to AI to draft a witness statement aimed at pushing the attacker's sentence as high as possible. And in a $21m (£16m) high court dispute between two Nigerian oil companies, a barrister relied on a supposed ruling by a senior judge that AI had fabricated entirely, prompting the judge to caution against using AI output without checking it.

### Real2SimLab: A Unified Platform for Real-to-Simulation Transfer
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34849

Researchers have introduced a new testing platform in a paper titled "Real2SimLab: A Unified Platform for Real-to-Simulation Transfer." Real-to-simulation transfer means constructing computer simulations based on what is observed in the real world. AI coding agents can now write simulation programs from video footage, and several benchmarks, or standardized tests, exist to measure them. The problem is that each benchmark sets its own inputs, expected outputs and scoring process, so testing many methods across many benchmarks requires a separate integration for every pairing. Real2SimLab tackles this by running each method's program and grading the resulting simulation with each benchmark's original scorer.

Source: CNBC
https://is.gd/ES9L4n

A hedge fund manager has warned that the artificial intelligence boom could end in recession, either because AI investment collapses or because the technology eliminates large numbers of jobs, CNBC reports. Sheru Chowdhry, founder and chief investment officer of DSC Meridian Capital, made the remarks last week at the iConnections Global Alts Asia conference in Singapore, during a panel moderated by CNBC's Joanna Ossinger. He said that if an AI-driven downturn does not arrive within a few years, one triggered by unemployment might follow instead. His concern extends beyond the possibility of an AI bubble bursting. ### S&P considers Nvidia credit downgrade over governance and ‘key man’ risks
Source: TechCrunch
https://is.gd/YSMoXW

S&P Global Ratings, one of the three biggest credit rating agencies, is weighing a downgrade of Nvidia, citing worries over the company's management and governance, TechCrunch reports. Although Nvidia's finances remain strong, S&P said the removal and subsequent return of chief executive Jensen Huang has pointed to "weak executive oversight and lack of succession planning," as well as key man risk, meaning the company leans heavily on Huang for its future. ### Data from Chinese AI firm MiniMax accessed by unidentified parties, researcher says
Source: The Economic Times
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow/136180952.cms

Confidential data sent to Chinese AI startup MiniMax was accessed by unknown parties, even after users asked that it not be stored or kept, a cybersecurity researcher told Reuters, the Economic Times reports. HD Moore, chief research officer at US security firm Dragos, named MiniMax as the unnamed Chinese developer in an earlier Dragos probe into AI data security. That investigation, published in a November 19 blog post, found that private web links included in requests to AI services were later opened by unauthorised parties. Moore said one secret link sent to MiniMax surfaced weeks afterwards in a chat channel on Feishu, the workplace messaging tool built by China's ByteDance, where devices in Beijing and Shanghai opened it. Source: The Economic Times
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/articleshow/136176738.cms

Business investment in Australia jumped again in the September quarter as companies spent heavily on data centres, posting its largest increase since 2011, at the peak of the mining boom, the Economic Times reports. Thursday's figures point to some resilience in the economy, even after five interest rate rises this year caused the deepest housing slump in 40 years, hurt consumer spending and softened the jobs market.
