# WORLD

### Claude writes machine-checked proofs for 123 open-source libraries
Lead: yes
Anthropic and the verification firm Theorem used Claude to produce formal, machine-checked proofs for 239 fuzzing entry points across 123 widely used open-source libraries, working at the level of compiled RISC-V code. The proofs show those entry points will not crash on inputs under 64 MB, under set assumptions such as single-threaded static binaries and modelled Linux behaviour, and the effort turned up bugs that had gone unnoticed for years. Anthropic says it does not prove the libraries are bug-free or correct on everyday x86 or ARM deployments. This is Anthropic's own account. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

Source: Anthropic https://www.anthropic.com/research/formal-verification

### Microsoft's diagnostic AI beats generalist doctors on hard cases, per Science
A Science paper on Microsoft's MAI-DxO, a system of AI agents that asks questions, orders tests and debates a diagnosis step by step, reports 83.2% accuracy on complex New England Journal of Medicine cases versus 36.1% for individual generalist physicians. It was a text-only benchmark, so it says nothing yet about specialists, team care, imaging or real patient outcomes. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

Source: Science https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aeh7930
Also: Microsoft AI https://microsoft.ai/news/building-medical-superintelligence/

# USA

### US military tests a robot strike team meant to go ahead of troops
At the request of US European Command, the Pentagon and DARPA tested four autonomous ground vehicles at Fort Knox that moved, coordinated and searched for targets on their own before a simulated rocket strike. The lead vehicle stopped and waited for instructions after spotting a possible enemy position, and it carried a drone that could scout or be armed. Planners see such robots taking the first blow if a NATO country is invaded. (Themes: technology trends; AI policy and regulation)

# INDIA

### India is building its own military AI layer
Defence Secretary Rajesh Kumar Singh said the Ministry of Defence is working with the IndiaAI Mission on a dedicated military AI layer for decision support, target acquisition and data fusion, and that the computing for it has already been procured. The work will lean on private firms already selected under the Mission. He said foreign suppliers could withhold such technology or build in backdoors or kill switches. (Themes: AI policy and regulation; technology trends)
