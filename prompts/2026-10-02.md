# WORLD

### DeepMind Introduces Watermarking for AI-Generated Biology
Source: Google DeepMind

Google DeepMind introduced SynthID Bio, a way to watermark AI-generated protein sequences and molecular structures. The mark is meant to show whether a sequence came from a trusted AI system, while leaving the biological function intact. As models begin proposing proteins, drugs, and enzymes, that signature is being presented as an early biosecurity layer for AI-designed biology.

### The Pentagon Plans an Autonomous Warfare Command
Source: U.S. Department of Defense

The U.S. Department of Defense announced plans for a new Autonomous Warfare Command. The command is aimed at scaling artificial intelligence, robotics, drones, and autonomous command-and-control systems across the military. The announcement treats military AI as an institutional priority, not a distant research project.

# USA

### FTC Opens a Probe Into Frontier AI Labs
Source: U.S. Federal Trade Commission

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission is opening an industry-wide probe into Anthropic, OpenAI, METR, and other frontier organizations over risks from autonomous AI systems. The questions are whether those systems can be controlled, what happens when an agent acts outside its instructions, and who is accountable if harm follows. It is one of the stronger regulatory signals yet from Washington.

### OpenAI Pulls GPT-6.1 Astra
Source: OpenAI

OpenAI decided not to release GPT-6.1 Astra after internal testing found shortcomings in scope control, authorization boundaries, and reporting behavior. The hold follows months of concern about agentic systems acting outside the job they were given. The decision shows a frontier model being delayed by safety requirements rather than by a lack of technical capability.

### Google Launches Gemini 4 Argon
Source: Google

Google introduced Gemini 4 Argon, a frontier model aimed at software engineering, cybersecurity, research, and long-context reasoning. Google says Argon is built for advanced reasoning and for stronger safeguards against prompt injection and misuse. The launch adds another contender alongside OpenAI, Anthropic, Meta, and xAI.

# CHINA

### DeepSeek and Huawei Build on Ascend Chips
Source: DeepSeek

DeepSeek announced a partnership with Huawei to build open-source infrastructure optimized for Huawei's Ascend AI chips. For years, large-scale AI training has depended on Nvidia's CUDA software. The partnership is part of China's push for a hardware and software stack that does not rely on Western platforms.

### OpenAI Reports an Attempt to Extract Model Reasoning
Source: OpenAI

OpenAI said it disrupted a coordinated effort to extract hidden model reasoning, and it linked elements of that activity to individuals associated with Moonshot AI. The disclosure points to a contest that is no longer only about building models. It is also about protecting how those models reason.

# ASIA

### A Tokyo Court Protects a Person's Voice
Source: Tokyo District Court

A Tokyo court ruled that a person's voice is protected under publicity rights, in the same way a face or likeness can be protected. The case involved allegations of AI-generated voice cloning. It is an early Asian precedent on synthetic media and digital identity, and other courts are likely to face the same question.

### South Korea Tests Generative AI in a Defense Network
Source: MakinaRocks

South Korean company MakinaRocks demonstrated generative AI inside the country's military command-and-control network during a major defense exercise. The test shows AI moving from civilian products into national security systems.

# INDIA

### Ajai Chowdhry Urges Sovereign Small Models for India
Source: Ajai Chowdhry

HCL co-founder Ajai Chowdhry argued that India should prioritize sovereign small models, local deployment, and early safety rules, rather than only chasing the largest and most expensive systems. The case he makes is for practical, efficient, multilingual models aimed at Indian problems.

### India Is Asked to Write Its Own AI Rules
Source: India policy discussion

Industry leaders, researchers, and policy experts are calling for an India-specific AI governance framework, rather than adopting Western standards unchanged. The themes they name are Indian-language tests, safety testing infrastructure, model provenance, accountability for deployment, and protection of citizens. The aim is for India to help write the rules, not only to consume foreign systems.
