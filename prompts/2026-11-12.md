# WORLD

### Rights groups urge a treaty on "killer robots" before next week's UN talks
Lead: yes
Three advocacy organisations want governments to start negotiating a binding legal protocol on autonomous weapons, with a firm deadline, when states meet in Geneva next week. Human Rights Watch says it is making the call together with Amnesty International and the Stop Killer Robots campaign ahead of the Convention on Conventional Weapons Review Conference, which is scheduled for 16 to 20 November. The conference meets just once in five years, so the groups see it as an unusual opportunity. They say weapons that can choose and strike targets without meaningful human control are already being tested and used. Many countries support a binding treaty. Leading military powers, however, have preferred voluntary guidelines, and discussions under the convention have been stuck for over ten years.
Source: Human Rights Watch https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/11/12/global-treaty-needed-to-combat-autonomous-weapons-threat

### Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40394

The researchers behind a paper titled Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks are tackling a weakness in how AI language models learn mathematics. Training material is usually chopped into isolated problems with answers. That loses the order of topics and the links between ideas found in the original books. In response, the researchers built TOME-Math. They drew it from 2,118 university-level textbooks and turned those into about 380 thousand proof records. The collection keeps chapters in sequence, pairs each statement with its proof, follows references between results and logs where every entry came from. After full training on this data, the model's average test score rose 16.7 percentage points above the base model, and it also beat three public training datasets.

# USA

### Google launches the Gemini Decisions API for fast yes/no and scoring calls
Google has unveiled Gemini Decision 1 along with a new Decisions API. The system is built to give quick verdicts rather than long written responses. In a single pass, the model can work through as many as 1,000 closed questions about one input. Those can be yes-or-no questions, multiple choice, or scoring against a rubric. Inputs can be text, images, audio, video or PDF files. Google says results come back with probabilities and confidence scores within 150 to 250 milliseconds. The price is ten cents per million input tokens, and output is free. The company is aiming it at tasks inside AI agents, such as routing requests, checking policies and grading work, where speed counts for more than a lengthy answer. The accuracy claims are Google's own.
Source: Google https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/gemini-decisions-api/

### DeepMind's AlphaGenome 2 predicts how DNA changes affect individual cell types
Google DeepMind has announced AlphaGenome 2, a model that forecasts how differences in DNA affect various human cell types. It was trained on over 100 million single-cell profiles drawn from 68 tissues. DeepMind says that, compared with its earlier model, it spots close to twice as many variants that alter gene expression in particular cell types. A single genetic variant can behave differently depending on which cell carries it, so predictions at the level of individual cells could help scientists work out how diseases develop. Researchers doing non-commercial work can use it through the AlphaGenome API, and commercial research has access through a private preview. The model is meant for research and is not approved for clinical diagnosis.
Source: Google DeepMind https://deepmind.google/blog/alphagenome-2-deciphering-how-cells-read-dna-to-better-understand-disease/

### Databricks launches an always-on Genie agent for enterprise work
Databricks has unveiled the latest generation of its Genie AI agent. It is designed to keep working on complex business tasks around the clock instead of simply answering questions from staff. The company says Genie can watch over data, handle longer assignments and bring findings to people without being prompted. A business ontology, essentially a structured map of the company, gives it context about an organisation's data, staff and processes. For administrators, Databricks describes several controls. These include restricting which tools the agent may use, keeping a record of each action it takes and capping its spending. The launch is part of a broader move away from assistants that wait for instructions and toward agents that work as operational coworkers, with governance designed in from the outset.
Source: Databricks https://www.databricks.com/company/newsroom/press-releases/databricks-launches-new-always-genie-autonomous-ai-complex

### United Airlines restricts third-party AI agents from booking flights
United Airlines says it is limiting outside AI agents from using its website and app to make bookings and other changes for customers. The airline points to risks involving account credentials, purchases customers never meant to make, and changes to reservations and loyalty points. It also cites rules that require a person to give certain acknowledgements, for example confirming baggage restrictions when checking in. The decision draws a practical line for agentic AI. Even if an agent is capable of booking a flight, a company will not necessarily let it make purchases for a customer. Before agents can shop for people, businesses will need clear policies on identity, authorisation, liability and human sign-off.
Source: United Airlines https://united.mediaroom.com/news-releases?item=125496

### Google releases A2UI 1.0, an open standard for AI-generated screens
Google has announced version 1.0 of A2UI, an open protocol that lets AI agents respond with interactive screens rather than plain text. The agent describes an interface, such as a form or a chart, and the app then builds it from its own native components. The result matches the look and feel of the rest of the app. The new version adds function calls that work in both directions, along with renderers for web and mobile frameworks. Instead of reading a wall of text and then doing the next step manually, users could be shown a screen to check, adjust and approve. Because the standard is open, agents and apps from other companies can use it as well.
Source: Google Developers Blog https://developers.googleblog.com/a2ui-10-build-delightful-fast-reliable-agentic-experiences/

# CHINA

### Baidu launches ERNIE Lightning and a video model at Baidu World 2026
Baidu used its Baidu World 2026 conference to unveil two new models, according to TPS Report. ERNIE Lightning is a reasoning model designed primarily for Baidu Search. ERNIE-Wizshot-ME turns a single photograph into a talking video with expressive faces and costs 0.7 yuan for each second of footage. Business customers can use both through Baidu's Qianfan cloud platform. Baidu claims Lightning lowers the cost of its inference engine, the system that runs its models, by over 30 percent on chips from Kunlunxin, Baidu's in-house chip unit. That figure comes from Baidu, which has released no benchmarks. The launches show Baidu's drive to power its AI with domestic chips while US export controls restrict access to Nvidia hardware.
Source: TPS Report https://tpsreport.news/news/baidu-ernie-lightning-wizshot-me-qianfan

### Beijing plans capacity for 200,000 robots a year by 2028
Beijing's industry bureau has released an action plan for embodied AI, meaning AI that runs physical machines such as robots, covering 2026 to 2028, EastFrontier reports. The plan calls for a minimum of five world-class humanoid robot manufacturers and no fewer than 200 robot-related firms. It also sets a goal of being able to produce 200,000 robots annually, and of a 50,000-petaflop computing cluster for training robot AI models. The plan is dated 11 November. China is betting big on humanoid and service robots as an emerging industry, and city governments are competing to attract the top manufacturers. Beijing is already home to several humanoid start-ups and held the first humanoid robot games. The plan aims to move from prototypes to mass production.
Source: EastFrontier https://eastfrontier.com/2026/11/12/beijings-embodied-ai-plan-targets-200-000-robots-a-year-and-200-firms/

# ASIA

### Japan weighs binding orders and penalties for AI firms after incidents
Kyodo News reports, citing a source, that Japan is considering legally binding orders, enforced with penalties, against AI companies that refuse to cooperate with the government after incidents involving advanced AI. A government working group is examining the country's 2025 AI law, which relies on voluntary cooperation and carries no penalties of any kind. An outline of the review is expected by the end of the year. Such a step would be a change of direction for Japan. Among major economies, it has so far taken one of the lightest approaches to regulating AI. The review comes amid rising concern about the misuse of powerful models and about cyber incidents linked to AI.
Source: Kyodo News https://english.kyodonews.net/articles/-/91104

# INDIA

### OpenAI now processes API requests inside India
OpenAI has begun processing API requests locally in India, The Week reports. For supported workloads, both the incoming request and the model's computation now take place within the country, which goes further than just storing data there. The service starts with GPT-6 Luna through the Responses and Chat Completions APIs. Supported customer content is also kept in India. The offering targets businesses that face rules requiring data to stay in the country, including banks and other financial companies. For regulated organisations, where data is processed can be as important as where it is stored. The change could therefore remove a significant barrier to using AI in sensitive business workflows.
Source: The Week https://www.theweek.in/wire-updates/business/2026/11/12/openai-to-process-ai-requests-within-india-for-businesses-with-data-localisation-needs.html
