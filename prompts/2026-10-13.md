### Cyber-attack similar to OpenAI Medicare breach seems likely in UK, says minister
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/12/cyber-attack-like-openai-medicare-breach-seems-likely-in-uk-says-minister

Kanishka Narayan, concerned by Australian incident, says British cyber defences could be bypassed by artificial intelligence agent An AI-driven cyber-attack similar to the OpenAI breach of Australia’s healthcare system is likely, “if not inevitable”, in the UK, according to a senior minister. Kanishka Narayan, the UK AI minister, indicated that the British government’s own cyber defences would be bypassed by a technology that has developed highly sophisticated skills in cybersecurity. In a speech Narayan said an incident in June when an OpenAI agent – a computer program acting autonomously – infiltrated Medicare, Australia’s universal healthcare system, could be repeated in the UK.

### AI less regulated than milk, Microsoft president says in warning over safety
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/12/ai-less-regulated-than-milk-microsoft-president-calls-for-tougher-rules

Artificial intelligence is less regulated than milk and washing machines, Microsoft president Brad Smith has said. He told the BBC that every carton of milk in a grocery store is subject to safety rules and inspection, while AI is not. Smith called for more safety rules, including a legal obligation to a victim if their system is breached by rogue AI agents, and said companies need emergency brakes, or kill switches, to pause AI that grows out of control. Microsoft chief executive Satya Nadella made a similar call on Saturday, writing that we must build contained systems whose behaviour we can observe and whose actions we can always contain. The comments came as Britain's AI minister prepared to urge frontier AI developers to do more on safety.

### This AI startup says it can generate permit-ready blueprints in minutes
Source: TechCrunch
https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/this-ai-startup-says-it-can-generate-permit-ready-blueprints-in-minutes/

Blueprints AI, a Santa Monica startup founded by architect Al Dram and data scientist Coco Cao, says its software produces code-compliant, permit-ready construction blueprints in minutes from a sketch or PDF. Dram says architects spend about eighty percent of their time drafting and only twenty percent designing, and he wants to flip that ratio. The pair trained their models on eight years of approved drawing sets from Dram's own design and construction firm. The first version costs between 3,200 and 8,500 dollars a month. At TechCrunch Disrupt this week, where it is part of the Startup Battlefield 200, the company will unveil a second version that drafts architecture, structural, mechanical, electrical, plumbing and interior plans for a building at once.

### World Bank jobs council urges 'crisis-era' employment support measures as IMF warns of AI ‘tsunami’
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/12/imf-world-bank-ai-jobs-inflation-.html

At the IMF and World Bank meetings in Bangkok, IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned that AI is hitting the global labour market like a slow-moving tsunami, with young graduates first in line, because the routine entry-level jobs they would normally start in are disappearing. The IMF estimates about forty percent of jobs worldwide will eventually be augmented, downgraded or eliminated, rising to sixty percent in advanced economies. Singapore's President Tharman Shanmugaratnam, who co-chairs the World Bank's Jobs Council, urged governments to make crisis-era support permanent, including incentives for firms to hire fresh graduates. World Bank President Ajay Banga was less pessimistic, saying smaller AI tools offer a large opportunity to build jobs and productivity in middle and lower-income countries.

### Intel, Broadcom CEOs warn capital crunch could become AI's biggest bottleneck
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/12/intel-broadcom-ceos-warn-capital-crunch-could-become-ai-bottleneck.html

Speaking at a conference in Singapore, Broadcom chief executive Hock Tan said access to capital may become a bigger constraint on AI than shortages of chips, memory or electricity. Intel chief executive Lip-Bu Tan agreed, pointing to the rising cost of capital and to investors who increasingly want to see a return on AI spending. Rising borrowing costs could make data centres and chip purchases more expensive to finance. Even so, both remain bullish on near-term demand. Hock Tan said demand for compute exceeds what the industry can supply and the gap could last through 2029, while memory and wafer shortages may ease as capacity grows. Both said long-term growth will depend on whether businesses adopt AI and see real economic benefits.

### AI may change the price of your Big Mac and groceries — what this means for shoppers
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/11/ai-dynamic-pricing-shoppers-groceries.html

Fast-food chains and supermarkets are rolling out AI tools that could affect what shoppers pay. A federal antitrust lawsuit filed against McDonald's this week alleges the company uses an AI-powered pricing engine to set menu prices across US locations, which McDonald's denies. Grocers including Kroger, Walmart and Whole Foods are adopting electronic shelf labels, and Kroger uses an AI platform to mark down perishables. Whole Foods says it does not use dynamic pricing, and the British Retail Consortium says UK supermarkets have no intention of using surge pricing. Experts warn, however, that more detailed customer data makes personalised pricing easier, and Bank of England economists have cautioned that firms could move closer to charging each shopper the most they are willing to pay.

### Sequoia-backed chip startup Nuvacore raising funds at about a $2.5 billion valuation
Source: The Economic Times
https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/tech/technology/sequoia-backed-chip-startup-nuvacore-raising-funds-at-about-a-2-5-billion-valuation/articleshow/134870940.cms

Six-month-old chip startup Nuvacore, which does not yet have a product, is raising hundreds of millions of dollars at a valuation of about 2.5 billion dollars to build a data-centre processor, according to two people familiar with the round. Demand for CPUs has grown with AI, because they act as traffic controllers for Nvidia's AI chips and run autonomous AI agents. Nuvacore was founded by engineers Gerard Williams, John Bruno and Ram Srinivasan; Williams previously sold his startup Nuvia to Qualcomm for 1.4 billion dollars. Its seed round was led by Sequoia Capital. Investors have returned to chip startups, putting about 10.7 billion dollars into semiconductor companies in the first five months of this year, according to Crunchbase.

### AI fuelling growth while putting Asia’s young workers at risk, IMF meeting hears
Source: South China Morning Post
https://www.scmp.com/week-asia/economics/article/3370618/ai-fuelling-growth-while-putting-asias-young-workers-risk-imf-meeting-hears?utm_source=rss_feed

Delegates at the IMF and World Bank annual meetings in Bangkok heard on Monday that a surge of investment in artificial intelligence may be helping prop up a global economy battered by energy supply shocks, but that its potential to cut jobs needs swift action if Asia's young people are to secure a place in the future workforce. Soaring energy prices, persistent inflation and mounting debt framed the opening of the week-long summit, the first held in the Thai capital since 1991. The South China Morning Post reports that oil prices have hovered around 100 dollars a barrel since the partial closure of the Strait of Hormuz, forcing governments to borrow heavily to cushion consumers.

### WindBorne wins a hefty US Air Force contract for its balloon data
Source: TechCrunch
https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/12/windborne-wins-a-hefty-us-air-force-contract-for-its-balloon-data/

Air Force will buy data collected by WindBorne Systems‘ fleet of autonomous weather balloons under a new contract that could be worth nearly $50 million to the startup. WindBorne currently has about 700 weather balloons flying around the globe, collecting atmospheric sensor readings that the company sells to customers and also feeds into its own AI weather forecasting model. WindBorne, founded in 2015 and based in Redwood City, California, is one of the first organizations to directly feed empirical data into a deep-learning weather model.

# CHINA

### China Mobile triples capacity at mega Inner Mongolia computing hub amid nation’s AI push
Source: South China Morning Post
https://www.scmp.com/tech/big-tech/article/3370600/china-mobile-triples-capacity-mega-inner-mongolia-computing-hub-amid-nations-ai-push?utm_source=rss_feed

State-backed China Mobile has tripled the capacity of its largest intelligent computing hub, in Hohhot, Inner Mongolia, as Beijing pushes digital infrastructure to power its AI ambitions. The expansion, which cost about 403 million US dollars, covers 44,500 square metres and has 120 megawatts of power capacity. Total processing capacity has risen to 20,100 petaflops from 6,700, according to Inner Mongolia Daily, and 16,700 petaflops of that is dedicated to artificial intelligence and smart computing. A petaflop is a quadrillion operations per second.

### How AI exposure varies across Indian states: Kerala leads, UP and Bihar lag behind
Source: Mint
https://www.livemint.com/ai/how-ai-exposure-varies-across-indian-states-kerala-leads-up-and-bihar-lag-behind-11791727545580.html

Kerala, Haryana, Maharashtra and Tamil Nadu have the highest shares of workers in occupations where artificial intelligence (AI) is more likely to improve productivity than replace human labour, while Uttar Pradesh, Bihar, Jharkhand and Madhya Pradesh face a double disadvantage of lower AI exposure and fewer opportunities to benefit from the technology, according to a new study by the Great Lakes Institute of Management. The October 2026 working paper, AI, Automation and India’s Workforce: Who Gains, Who’s Exposed, and How Policy Can Shape the Future, examines employment patterns across Indian states using labour force data from 2012 and 2025.
