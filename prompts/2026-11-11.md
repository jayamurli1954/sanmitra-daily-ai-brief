# WORLD

### Hackers used AI agents to steal Tajik government documents, researchers say
Researchers at the cybersecurity firm CloudSEK say a hacking group with links to Kazakhstan relied on autonomous AI agents to take more than 2,000 documents tied to national security from Tajikistan, Reuters reports. The agents were reportedly built on the open-source Hermes framework. They were used to scout targets, support the break-ins, write tailored software and organise the stolen material. The operation came to light because the attackers mistakenly left a server open that held their operational data. The findings point to AI in hacking moving past phishing and malicious code, toward agents that manage multiple phases of an attack, which makes defence harder.
Source: Reuters https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/kazakhstan-linked-hackers-used-ai-steal-tajik-government-documents-researchers-2026-11-11/

### World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation
Desk: Research Watch
Source: arXiv Pre-print
https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12885

In a paper titled World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation, the researchers tackle a tension in how robots learn. Skills from training usually sit in a network's weights, its internal parameters, and that binds them to the setting where training happened. In-context learning avoids this problem. It means picking up a task from examples given while the model is working. But it has trailed behind, because a demonstration recorded elsewhere reveals nothing about the robot's present surroundings. The researchers observed that this kind of learning captures meaning, like the steps of a task and what objects are for, rather than appearance, so it transfers between settings. In their method, a frozen model begins from a demonstration and practises the task in a simulator or similar environment. It holds the resulting feedback in its context rather than undergoing retraining.

# USA

### DeepMind's AlphaProof Code formally verifies parts of Signal's encryption
Lead: yes
Google DeepMind has unveiled AlphaProof Code, an agentic AI system that generated Lean proofs, checked by machine, for core security properties of the actual production code underpinning the Signal messaging protocol. The verified properties include forward secrecy and authentication, and the proofs reach right down to the compiled machine code. The work paired AI agents with mathematical proof methods and specifications written by people, producing proof code that runs to millions of lines. DeepMind describes it as a research proof of concept, noting that the proofs address selected properties rather than every conceivable attack. Even so, the company says it is a strong signal that AI could make formal software verification practical. That would mean going beyond bug-hunting to offer mathematical evidence that particular security guarantees hold, a development that could matter for cryptography and critical infrastructure.
Source: Google DeepMind https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-alphaproof-code/

### OpenAI opens ChatGPT to business profiles and business agents
OpenAI announced a beta called Businesses in ChatGPT, which allows companies to claim and control their profiles within ChatGPT. Using a Business Hub, qualifying firms can edit company information and provide product feeds with pricing and availability details. They can also view aggregated data, such as how often they are mentioned and how many clicks reach their websites. OpenAI is also launching Business Agents, which give customers a way to ask a firm's agent about what it sells and the services it offers. The initial rollout is limited to eligible e-commerce and online-service businesses in the US. OpenAI said businesses will not receive individual customer conversations. Firms may soon have to curate how they show up in AI assistants, as they already do on search engines and marketplaces.
Source: OpenAI https://openai.com/index/introducing-businesses-chatgpt/

That could shorten turnaround for media and marketing teams, with familiar creative software remaining central.
Source: Adobe https://news.adobe.com/news/2026/11/adobe-powers-new-era-of-creativity

### Fireworks AI details a security incident that reached secrets and model weights
Fireworks AI, which provides AI infrastructure, has released more details on a security incident in October that started at an outside cloud provider. The company said attackers got into internal development machines and viewed some account and billing information. In a limited set of accounts, they also reached secrets, datasets and model weights that were incomplete. Fireworks said the incident is contained, credentials have been strengthened and its investigation continues. It added that it has found no effect on its inference and training services, which by default do not retain customer data. The episode shows that AI security must extend across the full supply chain, from cloud hosts to datasets and development tools, not only the model itself.
Source: Fireworks AI https://fireworks.ai/security/bulletins/october-2026-security-incident-update

# ASIA

### Dassault Systemes to set up an ASEAN industrial AI lab in Singapore
Dassault Systemes, the French software group, has signed a memorandum of understanding with Singapore's Economic Development Board to establish its ASEAN Industrial AI Virtual Twin Lab in the city-state, TNGlobal reports. The lab will use AI and virtual twins, which are detailed digital replicas of products, factories and processes, for manufacturing and industry throughout Southeast Asia. Dassault intends to recruit more than 30 AI professionals at the lab over five years. The move strengthens Singapore's ambition to serve as the region's centre for applied industrial AI.
Source: TNGlobal https://technode.global/2026/11/11/frances-dassault-systemes-to-set-up-asean-industrial-ai-virtual-twin-lab-in-singapore/

### South Korea jumps to 10th in IMD's digital and AI competitiveness ranking
South Korea rose eight places to 10th out of 70 economies in the newest IMD World Digital Competitiveness ranking, the country's science ministry said, according to Yonhap. The ranking gauges how prepared economies are to take up digital technology and AI. The United States took first place. Singapore placed second in the Asia-Pacific region, China seventh and Japan 19th. South Korea's rise follows substantial government investment in AI computing, talent and public-sector use, as Seoul aims to rank among the world's top three AI powers.
Source: Yonhap https://en.yna.co.kr/view/AEN20261111002200320
