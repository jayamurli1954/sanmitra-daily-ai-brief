# WORLD

### CERN and Mistral team up on Flare for collider physics
Lead: yes
On 15 October CERN and Paris-based Mistral signed a statement of intent for a long-term partnership to train what CERN calls the first foundation model for collider physics — nicknamed Flare in reporting — on billions of simulated proton-proton collisions from the Large Hadron Collider. The goal is one pretrained "Large Physics Model" that can reconstruct collisions, identify particles, flag anomalies and help evaluate future detectors, instead of today's chain of specialised algorithms. CERN holds about one exabyte of LHC data and expects collision rates to rise roughly tenfold when the High-Luminosity LHC starts around 2030; no budget or delivery date was disclosed. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

### Simons, OpenAI and Anthropic put $15 million behind maths + AI
The Simons Foundation announced on 15 October a combined $15 million package with OpenAI and Anthropic to help mathematicians evaluate AI-assisted results, learn new tools and set community standards for AI use. Simons will manage awards and an open call for workshops and programmes; both labs will also supply tools and compute to Simons-backed researchers beyond maths into basic science. Foundation president David Spergel framed it as letting the maths community drive adoption rules rather than any single company. (Themes: AI industry and startup news; AI breakthroughs)

Axios reported that on Wednesday the Federal Trade Commission sent civil investigative demands — each more than 50 pages — to OpenAI and Anthropic seeking records on security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy policies, as part of a probe into possible consumer-protection violations. The agency also plans to compel executive testimony and later expand demands to third-party AI groups, a person familiar with the matter said. OpenAI said safety remains a priority and it will engage constructively; Anthropic's public reply was not quoted in the Axios scoop. ### Meta open-weights Muse Spark Open at 816B parameters
Meta's Superintelligence Lab released Muse Spark Open on 15 October: a natively multimodal mixture-of-experts model with 816 billion total parameters, about 42 billion active per token, and a 1-million-token context, aimed at coding and long-horizon agents. Weights ship under the Muse Model License with limited commercial terms; Meta says it is the first model in its class to run on a pair of Apple M5 Ultra Mac Studios via MLX, and APIs are live on Baseten, Fireworks, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together. Hugging Face hosts the collection; Meta says it met its Superintelligence Scaling Framework release thresholds after extra safety post-training. (Theme: new AI models and tools)

### Microsoft finishes pre-training MAI-Base-2
Microsoft AI said it completed pre-training MAI-Base-2, an MoE foundation model with about 2.8 trillion total parameters and 80 billion active, trained on 16,000 NVIDIA GB200 GPUs over 38 days on roughly 50 trillion tokens of organic (non-distilled) data including native vision. The lab claims 4.2–12.3× better pre-training FLOPs efficiency than DeepSeek-V4-Pro-Base on held-out bits-per-byte tests and lower BPB than other trillion-plus models it compared. The model is in post-training with no public release date; treat employee-circulated claims that it beat OpenAI's "Spud" checkpoint as unverified gossip unless Microsoft publishes them. ### Nvidia open-weights Nemotron 3.5 Super VL for agents
On 15 October Nvidia released Nemotron 3.5 Super VL, an open 120-billion-parameter multimodal MoE (about 12 billion active) for instruction following, tool use and document/image/video understanding, plus edge siblings Bolt 74B-A9B (now) and Bolt E4B (due 23 October). Nvidia says Super VL is up to about 5× faster than peers in its class on Artificial Analysis throughput and ships with open datasets, NeMo Gym RL environments and NIM packaging; partners named for post-training include Scale, Perplexity, CrowdStrike and Salesforce. Weights are on Hugging Face and ModelScope. (Theme: new AI models and tools)

# CHINA

### Beijing drafts export curbs on mega-parameter AI
China's Ministry of Commerce proposed adding 40 technologies to its export-control catalogue — 23 of them AI, plus solar and biotech — including model weights and source code for systems with at least 2 trillion parameters, or ranked among the world's top 10 on performance, usage or safety, and for models trained on "important data." The draft also covers AI agents, computing clusters and design tech for advanced AI and memory chips; open-source and public-domain research would be exempt, and overseas app/API serving of covered models would generally not count as an export. A temporary pre-export registration requirement took effect on 16 October; public comment runs to 15 November. Commerce framed the move as misuse prevention, not targeting any country. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)

### OpenAI pitches Daybreak cyber defence to Korean CISOs
In Seoul on 16 October OpenAI briefed about 60 corporate security chiefs on AI-era defence and showcased Daybreak, a package linking its models with Codex and Codex Security to find flaws, test exploits in a controlled setting and draft fixes. The pitch followed late-September breaches of internet-facing support systems at Shinhan, KB Kookmin, Hana and BNK Busan banks; investigators found traces of the open-source ARTEX AI penetration-testing platform on suspected attack servers, though how much AI drove the attacks is unconfirmed. OpenAI Korea CEO Kim Kyung-hoon said many major Korean firms have adopted or are considering Daybreak — a company claim. (Themes: AI industry and startup news; technology trends)
