# WORLD

### Australia's Ghost Shark drone subs will carry armed drones and sea mines
Australia's Defence department declassified on 19 October three payloads for its Anduril-built Ghost Shark autonomous submarines: swarms of Anduril Altius aerial drones that can scout or carry about 4 kg of explosives against vessels, up to 12 sea mines per run to close waterways, and supply caches for special forces. "Dozens and dozens" are being delivered under a A$1.7 billion contract signed last year; the first arrived in January and Ghost Sharks are this month in exercises with the US and UK off Southern California. Defence Industry Minister Pat Conroy said the drones "now have teeth", and the payloads are being offered to the US and UK under AUKUS Pillar Two. ### Interpretable AI model decodes how immune cells are recruited into tissue
A Nature Biomedical Engineering research briefing published 19 October describes SPACER, an interpretable neural network that learns the rules of cell recruitment directly from spatial transcriptomics data. Across several cancers it points to local antigen-presentation machinery and tumour mucins as drivers of T-cell recruitment into tumours, and in a mouse model of severe checkpoint-inhibitor myocarditis it flags CD4+ T cells as key disease drivers. This is peer-reviewed research, not a clinical tool. (Theme: AI breakthroughs)

Source: Nature Biomedical Engineering https://www.nature.com/articles/s41551-026-01803-7

# USA

### Anthropic commits 5% of its shares to a Public Benefit Fund
Anthropic said late on 18 October (US time) that, subject to stockholder approval, it will commit 5% of its shares to the Anthropic Public Benefit Fund, with another 2% added at the board's discretion for every 50% rise in company value above a baseline it has not disclosed. The fund is a wholly owned LLC, not a charity, so it can make large nonprofit donations or direct payments to specific groups; Anthropic named AI's impact on jobs as one example and said none of the equity will go to lobbying or political giving. With co-founder pledges and an employee match, it says about 20% of its shares are now set aside for public purposes. Reuters noted that Elon Musk replied by pledging one SpaceX share to "every kid in America" next year. (Themes: AI industry and startup news; AI policy and regulation)

### Tenstorrent raises $1.5 billion at a $6.2 billion valuation
Jim Keller's AI chip startup Tenstorrent said on 19 October it closed a $1.5 billion Series E at a $6.2 billion post-money valuation, more than double its December 2024 mark. Trace Capital and Innovation X led, with ARK Invest, Sandisk, Morgan Stanley, T. Rowe Price and Tether Investments among backers. The money will scale production of its Galaxy servers, which link dozens of its RISC-V-based AI chips. (Theme: AI industry and startup news)

### Microsoft pitches Project Perception to contain "agentic insiders"
In a 19 October post, Microsoft Security EVP Hayete Gallot argued that companies' own AI agents are now overprivileged "agentic insiders" that cannot be fully trusted, and said Project Perception, first announced in July, can discover agents, give each an identity and scoped permissions, and use "Guardian Agents" to supervise every action in real time. She also called for more model transparency, including chain-of-thought tokens, to judge whether an agent's intent matches its task. These are Microsoft's product claims. (Theme: new AI models and tools)

### Birla AI Labs open-sources Yug-1 for business forecasting
Birla AI Labs released Yug-1 on 19 October, a 271.8-million-parameter time-series foundation model on GitHub and Hugging Face for demand planning, equipment monitoring, finance and infrastructure, with zero-shot probabilistic forecasts. Founder Ananya Birla said it scored 0.744 MASE on GiftEval (lower is better), ahead of Google's TimesFM 2.0 but behind IBM's and Salesforce's models; these are company-shared results. ### Trade Marks Registry drafts AI disclosure rules for filings
India's Trade Marks Registry has proposed that every filing declare whether AI was used, with non-disclosure treated as misrepresentation; AI-suggested citations must be checked against official sources, and AI may not be used to write or alter affidavits or fabricate evidence such as labels, invoices or survey data. The Registrar could strike off filings and refer agents or advocates for discipline. This is a draft out for comment. (Theme: AI policy and regulation)
