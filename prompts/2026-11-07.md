# WORLD

### Lanarkshire AI datacentre to fully rely on National Grid despite renewables claim
Source: The Guardian
https://www.theguardian.com/uk-news/2026/nov/07/lanarkshire-ai-datacentre-datavita-chapelhall-national-grid-renewable-energy

DataVita, the company behind a major AI datacentre project near Glasgow, has conceded the development will run entirely on power from the National Grid, despite earlier promises of renewable supply, the Guardian reports. The firm, seen as central to Britain's AI strategy, had pledged that the Lanarkshire scheme would take 1GW from green sources, comparable to a small nuclear reactor or the country's largest windfarm. When questioned by the Guardian, DataVita said the arrangement depended on commercial agreements, planning, grid and consent processes. It has since quietly changed the plan, now describing the site, near Chapelhall outside Airdrie, as fully grid served. It says solar and wind farms might be connected later.

# USA

### Crusoe sells troubled Wyoming data center project to Google
Source: TechCrunch
https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/06/crusoe-sells-troubled-wyoming-data-center-project-to-google/

Crusoe, the Denver-based data center developer recently valued at 30.9 billion dollars, confirmed on Friday it has sold an unfinished project in southeastern Wyoming to Google, TechCrunch reports. Crusoe had previously tried without success to sign Google as a customer for the site, and Google reportedly voiced worries about costs and timelines under Crusoe's management. Crusoe first announced the project last year alongside Tallgrass, an infrastructure firm backed by Blackstone, with ambitions to reach 10 gigawatts of capacity. Google did not reveal the purchase price but said it plans to invest 17 billion dollars building out the campus. The sale underscores the struggle independent developers face in completing huge projects on schedule and on budget, amid difficulties obtaining equipment and power.

### Tech companies face mounting child safety lawsuits while pushing AI into schools
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/07/snap-social-media-teen-safety-lawsuits.html

Technology companies are facing a growing wave of child safety litigation even as they push artificial intelligence into classrooms, CNBC reports. Snap has reached a confidential settlement in a case alleging its technology caused serious psychological harm to two teenagers, after Meta, TikTok and YouTube earlier settled with the same plaintiffs. This week, Google was sued by parents who say their child experienced a psychological crisis after conversations with the Gemini chatbot inside Google Classroom. The concerns are mounting as AI firms chase trillion-dollar valuations and large internet companies spend heavily on advanced models for schools. Developmental psychologist Kate Blocker said engagement-driven features can harm users, calling for "broader systemic changes in tech design."
