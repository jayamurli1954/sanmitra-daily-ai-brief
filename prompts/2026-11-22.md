### OpenAI uses its models to improve semiconductor chip design
Lead: yes
Source: OpenAI
https://openai.com/index/advancing-chip-design-with-our-models/

OpenAI has described how its models have helped engineers improve its Jalapeño custom inference chip and work on third-party hardware.
One reported multiplier redesign reduced cell area by approximately 15% while reducing latency from two clock cycles to one. OpenAI says further design improvements have been silicon-proven.
The company is also working with Synopsys to integrate frontier AI models with electronic design automation tools.
**Why it matters:** AI is beginning to assist in designing the very hardware required to run advanced AI. This could accelerate chip engineering and improve computing efficiency.

### Southeast Asia’s data center boom leaned on Chinese demand. New U.S. rules put that pipeline at risk
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/18/nvidia-us-ai-chip-export-controls-data-centers-.html

Washington's latest export controls could put the brakes on Southeast Asia's rapid build-out of data centers, CNBC reports. Under an interim rule the Commerce Department issued on Monday, data centers based in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia must now obtain a license from the U.S. government before buying advanced artificial intelligence chips, Nvidia's among them. Operators whose facilities already host large Chinese clients have 90 days to seek that license, or they risk export restrictions down the line. The measure takes aim at the region's growing function as a supplier of computing power to companies from China. Construction of data centers across Southeast Asia has climbed sharply, driven by demand for the semiconductors used to train and build AI models. Because U.S. curbs keep Nvidia's top-tier chips out of China, Chinese tech groups such as Alibaba Group and ByteDance have turned to leasing capacity in data centers abroad.

# USA

### Lagarde says EU funds should take first losses on chip plants, data centers
Source: CNBC
https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/20/lagarde-eu-data-center-funding.html

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde said on Friday that public money from the European Union should absorb the first losses on large chip-plant and data-center projects, as a way to draw in private investment, CNBC reports. Speaking at the Frankfurt European Banking Congress, in her last address there before leaving the ECB in February, Lagarde said that where a challenge needs a European answer, the institutions able to deliver it should be reinforced. In technology, she argued, projects requiring European scale call for public-private partnerships at the European level, with public funds, including the EU budget, "taking the first losses and drawing in private capital." Lagarde has warned before that Europe lacks the data center capacity to meet its own needs, and that current plans for up to seven gigafactories across the European Union fall short.
